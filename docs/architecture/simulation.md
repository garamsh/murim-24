# Simulation

The simulation core: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- The simulation core is a C++ library in `core/`, with no engine dependency.
- A world is created from a seed, a map of buildable, rough and blocked cells, armies, structures, rules, villages, each faction's leadership and its rules of life. The rules are data: the separation people keep, the hostility between factions by their stances, the floors of the structure stages, the ticks in a day, and the sight within which people close on a foe. It advances one `tick()` at a time.
- Every NPC is a person: a position, health, a cooldown and a home village or none, its qi, its store of qi and the art skills it knows, the people it married and the children born to it, and what its birth gave it: the tick it was born, its family, its parents, its sex, its given name, its talent and attainment per discipline, its cultivation, a lifespan, and a soul or none; and the silver and wares it holds. A person's identity is its index among the world's people, kept for as long as the world runs, through every army it serves in and after it dies. A world holds at most `MAX_PEOPLE`, 500, living people. That is a hard upper bound, not a target.
- An army is a roster of the living people serving in it, never a count, with an objective, a faction and skills. Every living person stands on exactly one roster. A person who dies leaves its roster but keeps its record, so the dead cost no work on a tick and stay out of the spatial index.
- `conscript()` moves one living person from its roster to another army's. `disband()` returns every member with a home to that village's residents; members without one stay. Neither creates nor removes a person, so the people on rosters are conserved.
- Every army follows an order: an objective cell, and the skills, by index among its own, it strikes with. An army created from data follows its objective with all its skills. `order()` gives an army another order, which it follows from the next tick. That is the port through which a driver steers a body, whether the driver is Godot input or a test, and the same type and resolution serve an NPC and the player alike. A driver marks the armies it steers through `steer`, read by `steered`, saved and hashed; any driver may still order any army. The rules that decide for people on their own, diplomacy (below), decide nothing for a steered army's members. A test shows an unsteered leader declaring by the diplomacy and the same leader steered declaring nothing, and the mark surviving a save.
- The player's body is an army of one person, born into a village (`world-generation.md`). An army as a world is created with may name a village as its members' home without being its residents, as the player's body does; a home that is no village is refused. A test shows an army's member taking the village it names as home, a home past the villages refused, and an army naming none leaving its members homeless.
- A faction may have a leader, a person, and a succession rule, as data. The only rule is the duel; a faction without one keeps its leader. An order may name a faction to challenge, the same order for the player's body as for an NPC:
  - The challenger is the ordered army's lead. A duel taking members only refuses a challenger from an army of another faction, and a faction with no duel or no leader refuses every challenge.
  - The challenger and the leader become hostile to each other alone and fight by the one skill rule until one has no more than the duel's yield health left. The leader keeps the lead if both fall together.
  - A challenger who wins leads the faction. If the duel says so, every faction at peace with it by stance turns hostile to it, a change to the hostility by stance the world holds.
  - `challenge_refusal` returns why an army's lead may not challenge a faction's leader, a `ChallengeRefusal`, or `NONE` when it may. `order` refuses a challenge exactly when it names a reason, so the rule is written once. `duel` reads the duel being fought for a faction's lead: the challenger and the leader.
- Two factions stand by their stances, the rules' hostility, unless a declaration stands between them: war, or peace, held once per pair with the faction whose leader made it and the tick, read by `declaration`. `hostile` reads it first, war hostile both ways and peace neither, and the stances otherwise; every rule that asks whether one faction attacks another, the strike rule, a village's damage and repair, its raided state, a raid's target and a raid's end, asks `hostile`. `relation` is the one read of how a faction stands toward another, a `Relation`: its own; `ALLY`, neither hostile to the other by stance; `NEUTRAL`, at a peace declared; `HOSTILE`, either hostile to the other by stance; `AT_WAR`, at a war declared.
  - Only a faction's leader declares, through `declare`, at once and for both factions, the same order for the player's body as for an NPC. `declaration_refusal` returns the first reason it may not, a `DeclarationRefusal`, or `NONE`: `DEAD`; `NOT_THE_LEADER`, the person not leading the faction of the army it serves in; `SAME_FACTION`; `ALREADY_AT_WAR`; `ALREADY_AT_PEACE`, peace with a faction allied or neutral; `TOO_SOON`, within the diplomacy's `hold_days` of the pair's last declaration. `declare` refuses exactly when it names a reason.
  - Diplomacy is data, `Diplomacy` in `Life`, checked once a day, one rule for every leader no driver steers: toward each other faction with living people, in index order, a leader declares war on one allied or neutral with `war_chance` per mille a year while its own living people number at least `war_margin` per mille of the other's, and makes peace with one at a declared war with `peace_chance` per mille a year while its own are fewer; hostility by stance alone it leaves as it is. In the region a leader declares war outnumbering by half again at 1 in 10 a year, makes peace at 1 in 5, and every declaration holds five years.
  - Tests show, each with a control: a member refused for not leading and the leader's war declared, for both factions; each refusal in turn, `declare` refusing each, beside the order allowed, and war allowed once a day's hold has passed; an outnumbered leader at war making peace, where steered it does not; and a declared peace keeping a village from raiding a neighbour hostile by stance, and a declared war sending a raid against one at peace.
- Life is data, `Life`, given with the world. One function bears every person, the player's body and every NPC alike, at creation and on rebirth:
  - talent is a growth and a limit per discipline, each drawn uniformly over a mean plus or minus a spread; a lifespan in days is drawn the same way. A world given no `Life` draws no talent and ages no one
  - a strike that hits anyone or anything practises a discipline, its attainment there growing by its growth, up to its limit: an art skill's art's discipline, or for an army's skill the striker's best, its highest limit
  - a person dies, as a wound kills, once its age in days passes its lifespan plus `days_per_attainment` days for each point of attainment
- Everyone the world starts with has no parents. A village's first two residents of each of its households, `households` in `VillageSpec`, are born a man and a woman, married to each other, each heading a family of their own; everyone else the world starts with belongs to the family of the army it was created in. A village may name no more households than its residents pair into. A family branches only when one of its people founds a village (below). It was born `founding_age` days, drawn from `Life`, before the world began, so a birth tick is signed.
- Kinship is data, `Kinship` in `Life`, checked once a day; ages are in days and chances per mille a year, rolled from the seed:
  - a living person of marrying age, with a home and no living spouse, marries with the marriage chance a partner drawn among those free to marry, of the other sex and another family, whose home is the same village or one whose centre lies within the neighbourhood. Both record the marriage, in order
  - a wife of bearing age whose husband lives bears a child with the birth chance, no sooner than the spacing after her last, through the one birth function, into her husband's family, or hers if the data says so, at that parent's side, home and army
  - where the data balances sexes, the child takes the sex fewer among the living unmarried whose home is its village and who are not past marrying age, children included; otherwise, or when they are as many, its sex is drawn at even odds
  - no child is born while MAX_PEOPLE live
- Every person bears a name: its family's surname, drawn by the seed for each family as the world begins, and a given name for its sex, drawn at birth, each in Korean and romanised, from `Names` in `Life`. `name()` reads it.
- A soul is the history a person may carry from body to body: the lives it has lived and the disciplines it practised. `ensoul()` gives a living person one. The birth rule reads only that history, empty for a person without a soul:
  - a discipline practised in a past life draws its limit `discipline_widening / lives` further down and up, and every discipline `upper_widening / lives` further up, so the widening shrinks as lives repeat
  - a person carrying a soul who dies is born again on the next tick, in a village drawn by the seed among those with a living resident: into its first resident's family, with its first two residents as parents and that village as home, with a newborn's health. It serves in the army its body served in, which takes the residents' faction and skills and is bound for the birthplace. With no such village the soul is not born again
- The calendar is the core's: a day is `ticks_per_day` ticks, world data in `Rules` that is 1,800 in play, a season 30 days and a year 4 seasons. `date()` derives the year, the season and the day from the tick count, which `tick_count()` reads, the tick a person's `ready_at` is counted against, and `date_at` the same of any tick.
- Every person moves and strikes on every tick; there are no levels of detail. At 500 people the whole world costs less than the tick budget (below).
- People move toward their order's objective along their army's flow field, rebuilt when an order changes the objective. A step enters any cell the flow field reaches and never a blocked one. An objective must be reachable from where each member stands, when the army is created and when it is given an order.
  - the field is 8-connected: a straight step costs 5 and a diagonal step 7, and a diagonal step is open only where both cells beside it are passable, so no step cuts or grazes a blocked corner. Its distance is a path's cost over 5, in cells rounded down
  - a cell's step is the one, of those beginning a shortest path, whose cell lies nearest the straight line to the objective, a diagonal first among equals. A diagonal over open ground is walked on its line; a cell on no diagonal within a cell of it
  - a straight step moves `SPEED`, 16 units, and a diagonal step `DIAGONAL_SPEED`, 11, along each axis, so a person moves at about the same speed either way, 2.8% slower on a diagonal
  - tests show, each with a control: a diagonal walked step by step on its line, by a field and by a person at its speed, against a straight walk of 152 ticks; a walk to a cell on no diagonal within a cell of its line; and no step between two blocked cells touching at a corner, or beside one blocked cell, where open ground takes that diagonal
- People keep the separation, about a cell, from neighbours found through a spatial hash rebuilt each tick:
  - a person steps away from anyone closer than the separation, the harder the closer, and otherwise along its pull toward the objective
  - a person feels no pull within its arrival ring, as many cells from the objective as the square root of its rank in the army, so an arrived army spreads out to the separation
  - a person within two cells of its ring counts as standing still. A person on the move does not give way to people standing still, and they step aside for it, across the way it is going, so a standing crowd parts for someone walking out of it
  - a person on the move does not give way to anyone behind it, the side its pull points away from, so a band queues through a gap one cell wide while each of its people keeps the separation from the one ahead
  - a person on the move feels no pull while a living foe, someone it would strike, on the move itself, lies ahead of it along its pull within its reach, the longest range of its order's skills and the art skills it knows. It holds and fights rather than walking past, and walks on once that foe falls, stands or is no longer ahead; a foe behind it holds no one, so it can walk away, and a standing foe holds no one, so a steered band still walks in among a village's defenders; people no driver steers also engage foes, below. Holds are read from the pulls as they were, so the order people are read in decides nothing. A test shows two foes on crossing diagonals holding within reach and striking each other, one walking off once ordered back, where at peace the same two close to within a cell
  - a person no driver steers, with a living foe within the rules' `sight` cells, engages it: while a foe lies within its reach it feels no pull and stands to fight, standing still or not as before, and otherwise it closes on the nearest, the first in the crowd's order among equals, on the one of the eight steps nearest the line to it, open only as a flow field's step is, and counts as on the move. It keeps its objective when no foe is in sight, the step is not open, or its skills reach no farther than itself. A steered body never engages, so the player keeps its movement while its automatic strike still swings at foes in reach. The region's sight is 8 cells. Tests show a foe five cells away closed on and fought, where one seven cells away is left alone, and a steered body staying on its cell while the foe closes on it, where unsteered it leaves it
  - positions are read as the tick began, so the order people move in decides nothing
- A query visits the people it finds in place, with no result list to allocate or sort; every rule that reads it either adds up what it finds or breaks ties by index, so the order of the visit decides nothing.
- A skill is data: range, radius, damage to people, damage to structures, cooldown, qi cost and shape. A person's skills are its army's, followed by the art skills it knows; the army's share its cooldown, and each art skill keeps its own. A skill's shape, `Shape`, is the ground one use covers, aimed toward a point:
  - `AREA`, the disc of its radius around the point, brought within its range
  - `ARC`, a sweep: every point within its range of the user ahead of the way to the point, within a quarter turn either side, all around when the point is the user's own
  - `LINE`, every point within its radius of the line its range long from the user toward the point, however near the point
  - `SELF`, the disc of its radius around the user, wherever it is aimed
  - `TARGETED`, the one person aimed at and nothing else; a cast of it must name a person. No skill of the region's is targeted: it is kept for special skills
- One rule resolves every skill, for every person alike, the player's body included, with one branch by shape and none by skill:
  - a person given no cast draws by the seed among its order's skills, once their cooldown has passed, and the art skills it knows that are ready and that its qi pays for; one who knows no art draws as it always has
  - it faces the nearest living hostile person in range, the first in roster order among equals, and uses the skill toward it; a skill of shape `SELF` or range 0 reaches only its user, so it lands where the user stands, with or without anyone hostile near. The core keeps no facing: the way a strike faces is chosen on its tick
  - the use spends the skill's qi, and every hostile person its shape covers takes the damage once, raised by the edge of the user's home (below) and its realm's might, and for an art skill multiplied by its nature's potency (below), and every structure its shape reaches takes the structure damage once: a structure is reached where the shape covers the point of its footprint nearest the shape's own nearest point to the footprint's centre, or its centre for a disc
  - each use on a tick is recorded, by `strikes()`, with its user, its skill, its shape, where the user stood and the point it was used toward, for the view to draw. The record holds the last tick's uses, and is neither hashed nor saved
  - the region's basic strikes, the army's guard strike and each blade art's first skill, sweep an arc at arm's length, `MELEE`, a cell and a half, so a foe at the separation, straight or diagonal, lies within it; the dart art's first skill flies along a line 8 cells long and half a cell either side; the arts' wider skills land as discs at a point within the art's range, and mending and torching are self areas
  - tests show, each with a control: an arc sweeping both foes ahead within arm's length and none behind or beyond, where an area of no radius hits one, cast west sweeping the one there, and cast at itself all around; a line hitting every foe along it, where a shorter one stops short of the farthest; a self skill landing around its user wherever cast, where an area lands at the point; a targeted skill hitting the person it locks on alone and refused at a point or itself, where an area of its radius also hits the one beside and takes a point; each use recorded with its shape, origin and direction for a tick; a shape changing the hash; and a faction's strike reaching a foe a cell away and none two cells away
  - damage to structures is signed: a negative amount restores durability, up to the intact stage's floor, and never lowers a structure above that floor. Rebuilding is this rule, not a second one
  - damage to people lands after everyone has struck, and a person whose health reaches 0 dies
- A person may cast through the port, the player's body as any NPC: `cast` has it use one of its skills, its army's, not only its order's, or an art skill it knows, at a point, a person or itself on the next tick, in place of its automatic strike and through the same rule. The target gives the point the shape is aimed toward. A point beyond the skill's range is brought within it along the line from the caster, and a cast at itself is aimed at the caster, whom its shape spares. Its cooldown starts even when it hits no one. A later cast before the tick replaces it, and the tick clears every cast:
  - `cast_refusal` returns the first reason it may not, a `CastRefusal`, or `NONE`: `DEAD`, `NO_SKILL`, `ON_COOLDOWN` for one not ready on the next tick, `NO_QI` for one costing more qi than the caster holds, `NO_TARGET` for a dead person or one the world lacks, or a target that is not a person for a targeted skill, and `OUT_OF_RANGE` for a person beyond the range. `cast` refuses exactly when it names a reason, and a cast it names a reason for on its tick lapses
  - `aim` reads, with no change to the world, the hostile person a skill would aim at: the nearest in range ahead of a facing it is given, within a quarter turn either side, else the nearest behind. With a facing of (0, 0) it is the automatic strike's aim. The core keeps no facing
  - tests show, each with a control: a cast at a far person landing there and sparing the nearer; one at a point past the range landing at the range, where a shorter skill falls short; one at itself hitting those in its disc and sparing the caster and those outside it; each refusal, `cast` refusing it, beside the cast allowed, and the same cast allowed once the cooldown passes; an NPC's cast landing, and its order's skill alone hitting no one; a later cast replacing the first; a cast lapsing after its caster's conscription; casts replayed giving the same hash, and a tick later another; and `aim` taking the foe ahead of the facing, and the nearest with none or with no one ahead
- Qi is data, `Cultivation` in `Life`, one rule for every person. Each birth draws a growth a day and a limit; once a day a person's store grows by its growth up to its limit, and every `recovery_ticks` ticks its qi recovers `recovery` per mille of its store, rounded up, up to the store. The people a world starts with begin cultivated for their founding age, and full. Without cultivation no one holds qi.
- Realms are data, `Life.realms`, ascending by qi, each a name and a might per mille. A person's rank is how many of them its store reaches, 0 for none, derived on read by `rank`. Its might multiplies the body damage it strikes with.
- Arts are data, `Life.arts`: each a name, the discipline its skills practise, a nature of orientation, orthodox, unorthodox or demonic, and polarity, yang or yin, and its skills, each with the rank needed to learn it and whether comprehension can unlock it. A person's known skills are a list, append-only, in the order learned. Its nature is that of the art it learned first, its heart method, read by `nature`. `Learning` in `Life` gives, for each axis on which an art differs from the heart, an ease per mille, multiplying learning chances and dividing a reading's cost, and a potency per mille, multiplying the art's body damage. A person learns a skill of an art three ways:
  - comprehension: on each realm gained, for each art it knows, it rolls `comprehension` per mille, scaled by its talent limit in the art's discipline over that discipline's mean limit, to learn the art's first comprehensible skill it lacks and has the rank for
  - a manual lying on a cell: each tick the nearest living person within `reading_reach`, who has the rank for a skill of its art it lacks and the qi, spends `reading_cost` qi and learns every such skill; the manual stays
  - teaching: once a day, a member of a sect whose seat is held learns, with `teaching` per mille a year for each rank of its highest-ranked elder, the first skill it lacks of an art its sect holds a manual of, within both their ranks
- `skill_count`, `skill_of` and `ready_at` read a person's skills, and `realms` and `arts` the data.
- Tests show, each with a control: a store growing a day up to its limit and qi recovering, against a world with no cultivation; founders cultivated for their founding age; a rank from the thresholds reached, a tick short of one the realm below; realms descending, a might past its bound, an art of an unknown discipline, a skill of a rank past the realms or a cost past its bound, recovery every 0 ticks and an ease past a thousand refused; a manual on a cell teaching what its reader's realm allows, read again once its realm grows and staying, where a reader short of the qi learns nothing; reading spending its cost and the second manual left for want of qi, read when the qi suffices; an art of another nature costing four times as much at half the ease on each axis; might and potency scaling a cast's damage, and the heart's own art striking whole; an art skill cooling down while another and the army's are ready; a cast refused for lack of qi beside one within it; a use spending its cost, and a tick without one spending none; a reader striking a foe unbidden with an art, where one who learned none hits no one; a realm gained comprehending a skill, where no chance or a skill comprehension cannot unlock leaves it unlearned; a sect teaching its members its art and not one it holds no manual of, where a sect teaching at no chance teaches no one; and known skills changing the hash, where the same reading hashes the same.
- A structure is a footprint of width x height cells from its top-left cell, and a durability. One cell is the 1 x 1 footprint, with no rule of its own. Structures are found by area through a spatial hash of their footprints' centres, queried as far beyond a disc as the widest footprint reaches, and a disc hits a structure when the footprint's nearest point lies within it. Its stage — intact, damaged, destroyed or rubble — follows from durability by the floors in the rules.
- A village is data: its structures and the army living in it, its residents, whose members make it their home. Its owner is its residents' faction. While its residents have a living member, it takes damage only from factions hostile to its owner, and repair only from the others. Once none lives, it takes either, and the first army to restore one of its structures becomes its residents, and its members' home, so the owner becomes that army's faction.
- A village holds food, and what each of its structures holds is data, `Holding`: the people it houses, the food it stores, and the food it yields each harvest, which makes it a field. With husbandry, `Husbandry` in `Life`, once a day:
  - on the first day of winter the village adds its intact fields' harvest to its stock, which never holds more than its storage; it starts with its storage full
  - it feeds everyone whose home it is the consumption a day from its stock, or, when it cannot feed them all, none of them
  - a person unfed for the hunger days moves, by the seed, to a village within the migration reach with room under its cap and food in store, joining its residents, or dies
- A village's cap is derived on each read and never stored, in people: the least of the housing of its structures still standing, intact or damaged, the yearly harvest of its intact fields over a person's yearly consumption, and the storage of its standing structures over the same. Without husbandry it is MAX_PEOPLE. A birth's chance is scaled by the headroom of the child's village, (cap - population) / cap, so births slow near the cap and stop at it.
- A workshop is a structure whose `Holding` also gives the people it arms or tends and what it does for them: an `edge` per mille on body damage, or `healing`, health a day. A village's arms and tends are the growth's `self_reliance`, the people it serves with no workshop, plus the arms, or tends, of its standing structures. While they cover the people whose home it is, those people strike with the greatest edge among its standing structures, a strike's body damage multiplied by (1,000 + edge) / 1,000, and once a day recover the greatest healing among them, up to `newborn_health`. A ruined workshop does neither. All of it is derived on read.
- Tests show, each with a control: residents striking half again as hard beside a smithy intact or damaged, and as before once it is destroyed or arms fewer than them; the wounded healing a day's healing beside an apothecary intact or damaged, up to a newborn's health and never lowering anyone above it, and not at all once it is destroyed or tends fewer than them; and a village with self-reliance below its housing laying a smithy, then an apothecary, where one with self-reliance equal to it lays a house.
- Villages grow by one rule, `Growth` in `Life`, checked every `interval` days, with no branch per faction. A village with living residents presses against its cap once the people whose home it is reach `pressure` per mille of it. It then takes the first expansion of its owner's stance, per-faction data, that it can:
  - build: while every structure of its own is intact, it lays the plan, among those it builds, that most raises its scarcest holding counted in people: housing, food, storage, arms and tends, in that order among equals. The plan is laid as rubble, on the free buildable cells within `vicinity` of its centre nearest it, a cell clear of any structure, village's clearing or unbuildable cell. A clearing is land a village keeps clear as though a structure covered it, such as a seat's court, given with the village as `VillageSpec`'s `clearings`. Its residents raise it by the repair rule, and it houses, stores, yields, arms or tends once it stands. A village whose other holdings exceed its self-reliance thus builds a smithy, then an apothecary; by default self-reliance is as many as a `std::uint32_t` holds, and growth builds no workshop
  - found: with more residents than its settlers and none still settling, it lays the settlement's layout as rubble on free buildable land drawn by the seed. The land's centre lies within `reach` of its own, `spacing` cells clear of any structure or village's clearing, and held by no other faction. The settlers march there as the new village's residents: a founder, the first resident of the lineage's sex who leads no faction, its living spouse and unmarried children, then others in roster order. The founder and its children take a new family, a branch bearing its surname. The layout makes a village of the growth's `settlement_kind`, and `settlement()` reads the land a village was founded on: that kind, the settlement area and its corner; nothing for a village the world was created with
  - raid: with more residents than its raiders and no party out, it sends the first raiders who lead no faction against the nearest village of a faction it is hostile to, with living residents, within `reach`. The village keeps that army as its party and reuses it. The raid ends once the party has no one left, the target has no living resident, or its owner is no longer one the party is hostile to, and its members return to their homes. A party that becomes another village's residents by restoring a structure there is no longer the village's
- Settlers keep their old home until a harvest finds their new village's cap holding all its living residents, which then makes it their home.
- A cell lies in the territory of the owner of the nearest village with living residents whose centre lies within `claim` cells, derived on each read. `territory()` reads it.
- Growth lays a structure only where it covers no army's objective and no living person, and only if every person can still reach its army's objective and the settlers their new centre. Laying one that blocks rebuilds every army's flow field. `plan_of` reads the plan a structure was laid from, and `army_count`, `structure_count` and `village_count` count what growth added.
- A field shows its season, sown in spring, growing in summer, ripe in autumn and fallow in winter, and is burnt whenever it is below its intact stage. A torch burns it by the one strike rule, and mending restores it.
- A village's state is derived on each read and never stored:
  - ruins while every structure is rubble
  - otherwise raided while a living person of a faction hostile to its owner could damage one of its structures from where it stands: a skill that damages structures, its range and radius away
  - otherwise prosperous while every structure is intact, and rebuilding while one is not
- A village records who ruined it: the army, and its faction, whose strike brought its last standing structure to rubble. The record outlives rebuilding and is part of the hash.
- A structure's footprint blocks movement for as long as the world runs, rubble included, unless its kind does not block, as a field does not. Flow fields change only when growth lays a structure that blocks. Footprints must lie on passable cells, cover no other structure, and leave every person's start uncovered.
- A cell is buildable, rough or blocked: bodies cross a rough cell, and nothing is built on one. `Map::buildable` is the one test of where something may be built, which growth reads here and the region's generation reads for its villages. A test shows a house laid off rough cells that open ground would have put it on, and another a house laid a cell clear of a clearing over the cells it would otherwise take.
- A sect is data given with the world, `SectSpec`: a name, a faction and a seat, a village by index, with founders who join it at a starting attainment in their discipline of highest talent limit. Membership is the person's `sect`, and everything else about a sect is derived on read:
  - its members are its living people
  - its elders are the `elder_share` per mille of its members of the highest best attainment, from its faction's `Admission`, the lower index among equals, and at least one while it stands; its master is the elder of the highest, the lower index among equals
  - its seat is held while the village is not in ruins and its owner is the sect's faction, and its cap is then the `pupils` of the seat's standing structures, and 0 otherwise
  - it stands while it has a living member
- Once a day, after kinship, sects are kept:
  - a member serving in an army of another faction leaves its sect
  - each manual a fallen sect holds, of an art, scatters, with `Manuals`' chance, to a passable cell drawn by the seed within its reach of the seat's centre, and is otherwise lost; a manual's place is stored, a sect, a cell or lost
  - a living person of no sect, with a home, of a faction with an `Admission`, old enough, with a talent limit and an attainment at its thresholds in one discipline, whose home lies within its reach of a sect of the faction with its seat held, a living elder and room under its cap, joins one such sect drawn by the seed, with the admission's chance a year
- A person may apply to a sect through the port, the player's body as any NPC: `apply` makes it a member at once when the daily admission's thresholds hold for it and that sect, without the chance or the draw. `application_refusal` returns the first that fails, an `ApplicationRefusal`, or `NONE`: `DEAD`, `IN_A_SECT`, `NO_HOME`, `NOT_OF_FACTION`, `TOO_YOUNG`, `UNPROVEN`, for talent and attainment together, so a refusal never tells hidden talent, `TOO_FAR`, `SEAT_LOST`, `NO_ELDER` and `FULL`. `apply` refuses exactly when it names a reason, and the daily admission assesses every person by the same function.
- A faction with an `Election` whose leader is dead or missing, or on a day its term divides, is led by the master of its sects with the most elders, each elder favouring its own sect's master, ties to the higher best attainment and then the lower index; with no master it keeps its leader. It has no duel, so every challenge on it is refused. `elects` reads whether a faction has an election.
- The world records events in a ring of the latest `EVENT_CAPACITY`, 64, read oldest first by `events()`: each its sequence, counted over every event recorded, its tick, its cell and what happened, a `Happening`: a village founded, with its faction; a party sent against a village, with its owner and the raiders' faction; a village ruined, with its owner and destroyer; a sect fallen, with its faction, noted when its last member leaves; a faction's lead changing hands, by election or by duel, and not a lead renewed; a person born, a child or a soul born again; and a person dying, by a wound, age or hunger. The cell is the village's or the seat's centre, or where the person stood.
- The world notes each step of people's progress in the martial arts apart from the events, in a store of the latest `PROGRESS_CAPACITY`, 32, read oldest first by `progress()`: each its sequence, counted over every step noted, its tick, and what it was, an `Advance`: a skill learned, with the person, the art, the skill's index in it and how, by comprehension, a manual or its sect's teaching, one step for each skill; or a realm reached, with the person and the rank, one step for each realm. No rumour is derived from it. The store is part of the hash, folded only while it holds a step, and of a save, which refuses a step naming a person, art, skill or realm the world lacks.
- `rumours` derives the events kept that reach a person, newest first: those within the rules' `rumour_reach` cells of where it stands, along each axis, and wherever they happened, the births and deaths of its kin, its family, spouses, parents and children, and what befell its faction, a village of it founded, raided or ruined, one raided or ruined by it, a sect of it fallen or its lead changing hands.
- One person may talk to another within the rules' `talk_reach` cells, along each axis: 2 in the region. `talk_refusal` names the first reason it may not, in order: either is dead, the two are one person, the other stands beyond the reach, or their factions are hostile or at war as `relation` reads them. `offers` lists what a person offers one who talks to it, in order: its news, `Offer::NEWS`, while it lives, then its wares, `Offer::TRADE`, while it sells any, and nothing once dead. The player hears rumours by talking, the news being the person talked to's own `rumours`.
- Tests show, each with a control: a person within the reach talked to and one a cell beyond it refused; a person of a faction hostile by stance refused both ways, where the same person of an allied faction talks; one of a faction at war refused, where before the declaration it talks, and one of a faction at peace made talking, where hostile by stance it refused; the dead neither talking nor offering, where the living one beside them still offers its news; a talk reach outside [0, MAX_REACH] refused, where the limits are accepted; and a saved world keeping its talk reach, where another reach saved leaves the same two out of reach.
- Silver and wares go round by one rule, `Trade` in `Life`, for every person alike, the player's body included:
  - a ware is data: its name, its price in silver, the health it restores, up to `newborn_health`, the qi it restores, up to the consumer's store, and the discipline whose people sell it. The region's are a healing herb, 10 silver for 30 health, and the Lesser Return Pill, 60 silver for 2,000 qi, both sold by medicine
  - `sells` is the one rule of who sells what: a living person whose best discipline, its highest talent limit, is the ware's
  - each birth holds the trade's purse of silver, 30 in the region, and none of the wares; once a day every living person with a home earns the wage, 1 in the region. Silver is a plain count on the person; the dead keep theirs
  - `buy` takes a ware's price from the buyer to the seller at once, refused by `buy_refusal`'s first reason: either is dead, the buyer may not talk to the seller, as `talk_refusal` says, the seller does not sell it, or the buyer holds less silver than its price
  - `consume` uses a held ware at once, refused by `consume_refusal`'s first reason: the consumer is dead, holds none of it, or would gain nothing, at `newborn_health` or restoring no health, and at its store or restoring no qi
  - every person no driver steers, once a day, buys one of each ware it holds none of from the first person it may buy it from; and each tick, after damage lands, consumes the first ware it may that restores health while its health is at most the trade's `wounded` share of `newborn_health`, half in the region, and the first that restores qi while its qi is at most the `drained` share of its store, a quarter
- Tests show, each with a control: a person selling the wares of its best discipline and not another's; no one selling without a trade; a purchase paying the seller and adding the ware; each refusal of a purchase, `buy` refusing it, beside the herb sold within reach and the pill sold to a buyer with its price; the dead neither selling nor buying nor consuming; a herb restoring health up to a newborn's and refused at full health, where held while wounded it is consumed; a pill restoring qi up to the store and refused while full; a person no driver steers buying a herb on its first day and consuming it the next tick, where steered it does neither; a wage a day to those with a home, where a person without one earns none; silver and wares part of the hash; a trade outside its limits refused, where the limits are accepted; and a saved world keeping its silver, wares and trade, where one herb fewer bought hashes otherwise.
- Tests show, each with a control: an applicant joining at once where a chance of none admits no one in two years, and each refusal in turn, `apply` refusing each, beside the same villager admitted; every death recorded where and when it happened, and the soul born again on that tick after it; a sect's fall recorded once, and none while a member remains; a lead won by election recorded once over days that renew it, and one won by duel, and none for a duel lost; a village founded, a raid sent and a village ruined recorded, and none at peace; the ring keeping the latest 64 of 70 deaths, and every one of 63; and a sect's fall reaching its faction's villager from afar and not an outsider beyond the reach, who hears it within one, and a death reaching the dead one's family and not a stranger of its faction. Also: every realm a reader reaches and every skill it reads from a manual noted, where a reader short of the qi reaches the same realms and is noted learning nothing; a skill comprehended and the skills a sect teaches noted with how, where no chance of either notes none; and the store keeping the latest 32 of 40 realms reached, recording no event for them, and every one of 31.
- `sect_count`, `sect_of`, `sect_name`, `sect_faction`, `sect_seat`, `seat_held`, `sect_members`, `sect_elders`, `sect_master`, `sect_cap`, `sect_stands` and `manuals` read sects and manuals.
- Tests show, each with a control: admission of a faction's people within reach up to the cap, and of no one below a threshold, beyond reach or of another faction; a lost seat, ruined or held by another faction, capping its sect at 0 and keeping its members; a member conscripted into another faction's army leaving, and one into its own staying; elders as the share of members of the highest attainment, at least one, and the next elder becoming master when the master leaves; a fallen sect's manual scattering within reach, to the same cell for the same seed, or lost, and a fallen sect admitting no one; and an election making the master leader when no one leads, and `elects` reading a faction with an election as electing and the same faction without one as not.
- All simulation state is integers: positions are fixed-point at 1/256 of a cell.
- A world exposes a hash of its whole state. Determinism is tested by comparing hashes: the same seed gives the same hash after 10,000 ticks with armies moving, fighting, losing people and breaking structures. A test pins that hash, so it must be the same on every compiler and platform; it folds each person's sect, the sects, the manuals, each faction's admission and election, the rumour reach, the talk reach while it is set, and the events kept. A person's qi, store and cultivation are folded only in a world whose people cultivate, its known skills only once it knows one, and a skill's qi cost only when it has one, so the pinned hash holds. The hash covers every person, living or dead, with what its birth gave it and the cast it was given, folded only while one is pending, the souls, each army's roster and order, and the villages, and a log of orders and casts, each with the tick it was given before, replays to the same hash. It folds the declarations, the steered armies and the diplomacy only while each is held, a person's silver and wares and the trade's data only in a world with a trade, a skill's shape only when it is not an area, and the sight only while it is set, so a world with none keeps the pinned hash.
- A world is saved through the save port, `SaveStore`, which keeps bytes by `Slot` and names no file or technology. `save` writes a `Saved`: the world's whole state, its data included, and for a world made from a generated region its `Origin`, the region's seed and the village the player was born into. `load` reads it back and rebuilds what the world derives, its flow fields and structure index. The encoding is the core's own: little-endian fixed-width integers under a magic, a format version, the payload's length and a checksum.
  - `load` returns the world or a `LoadError`: `MISSING` for an empty slot, `NOT_A_SAVE`, `UNSUPPORTED_VERSION` for a version other than `SAVE_VERSION`, older or newer, `TRUNCATED`, and `CORRUPTED` for a failed checksum, trailing bytes, a tag out of range, or a state no world can be in: a reference to a record it lacks, a living person on no roster or on two, or data outside the constructor's limits
  - any change to what a save holds raises `SAVE_VERSION`
  - tests show, each with a control: a generated region lived 10 years at days of five ticks loading with its hash, and with the same hash after 1,000 more ticks, where another seed's differs at both points; a flipped byte, a save cut at each part, trailing bytes, a wrong magic, another version and an empty slot each refused with its error, beside the intact save loading; a save resealed with a valid checksum naming a plan the world lacks refused, where the same save resealed unchanged loads; the same of an event naming a person the world never held, where unchanged it loads with its events, and of a pending cast at such a person, where unchanged it loads with its cast; the same of a step of progress naming a realm past the world's, where unchanged it loads with that step; and a region lived 10 years loading with every skill learned and realm gained its store holds, as noted; and a pending cast loading with its hash and landing as the saved one does, where a cast at itself hashes otherwise; and a steered army and a declared war loading with their hash, the war still within its hold, where the same world unsteered and at peace hashes otherwise; and a talk reach loading with its hash, where another reach hashes otherwise
- The world ticks 30 times a second, so a rate given per second, such as skills, becomes work per tick.
- The tick budget is a p99 tick time of at most 4 ms on the development Mac (Apple Silicon), on the M1 benchmark scenario in `core/m1_scenario.cpp`, the worst case: MAX_PEOPLE people, every one in one battle of two lines with a barricade between them, and two caster armies breaking it. Its people close on foes within 8 cells, as the region's do, so the lines meet at arm's length through the gaps between the barrels. On 2026-10-09 it measured a tick p99 of 1.642 ms at 18.4 strikes a tick, where `main` before ADR 0046 measured 0.578 ms at 16.0. The benchmark fails its setup when the battle strikes fewer than 16 times a tick, so a scenario that stops bounding a battle in play does not pass. `make bench` measures it. Timing depends on the machine, so `make ci` does not run it.
- `make sweep` lives the generated region of each seed in a range, 0 to 49 by default, for a number of years, 100 by default, with days of five ticks. Per seed it prints the households each faction starts with, the living and the peopled villages at the start and the end, the peak, whether every faction kept a peopled village at the end of every year, the living at each realm rank and those knowing an art at the end, how many people ever learned from a manual lying on a cell, and how many wars leaders declared and peaces they made. Over seeds 0 to 49 for 100 years it measured 50 wars and 25 peaces, every faction peopled on 48 seeds, with people fighting at arm's length (ADR 0046). It tunes data and gates nothing, so `make ci` does not run it.
- The core is built, tested, linted and formatted with the toolchain ADR 0003 chose. Lint parses it on every host against one pinned Ubuntu 24.04 sysroot, unpacked by `lint-sysroot.sh`, so local and CI lint read the same standard library.
- `age_in_years` reads a person's age in whole years of the calendar, and `faction_of` the faction of the army a person serves in, none once dead. Both read what the world holds and change nothing.

## Rationale

- The core is C++ and loads no engine, so its rules run headless: ADR 0002 (`adr/0002-build-on-godot-with-a-cpp-simulation-core.md`).
- The build, test framework, formatter and linter, and how CI installs them: ADR 0003 (`adr/0003-build-the-core-with-cmake-googletest-and-llvm-tools.md`).
- Lint against one pinned Linux sysroot, because clang-tidy's findings depend on the standard library it parses: ADR 0016 (`adr/0016-lint-against-one-pinned-linux-sysroot-on-every-host.md`).
- An 8-connected flow field that never passes beside a blocked cell, each cell's step the straightest of a shortest path, diagonal steps at about the straight speed, and a person on the move holding before a foe on the move within its reach: ADR 0037 (`adr/0037-move-people-eight-ways-and-hold-them-before-a-foe-in-reach.md`), which supersedes ADR 0005's 4-connected breadth-first search.
- A person on the move ignoring people behind it, so a crowd passes a gap: ADR 0020 (`adr/0020-let-a-person-on-the-move-ignore-people-behind-it.md`), which supersedes ADR 0017's rule that people on the move give way to one another.
- Every NPC a person with an identity, armies as rosters, conscription and disbanding, no levels of detail, the population bound, and the movement rule: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`), which supersedes ADRs 0005 and 0007 and ADR 0006's multiplicities.
- Flow fields, the spatial hash, and fixed-point numbers for determinism across builds: ADR 0005 (`adr/0005-simulate-armies-at-three-levels-of-detail-in-fixed-point.md`), whose tiers and aggregates ADR 0017 removes.
- Skills as data, the single rule that resolves them, and structures and their stages: ADR 0006 (`adr/0006-resolve-every-skill-through-one-rule-scaled-by-count.md`), whose scaling by count ADR 0017 removes.
- Villages, their derived state, repair as a signed structure effect, owners following residents, and the recorded destroyer: ADR 0015 (`adr/0015-run-the-village-life-cycle-from-structures-and-residents.md`).
- Footprints, the hit rule they share, footprints that block in every stage, and objectives that must be reachable: ADR 0013 (`adr/0013-give-structures-footprints-that-block-movement-in-every-stage.md`).
- Leaders as people, the duel as faction data fought by the one skill rule, and challenges through the order port: ADR 0018 (`adr/0018-change-a-factions-leader-by-duel-as-faction-data.md`).
- Growth by one rule of stances and pressure, building and founding by laying rubble raised by repair, family branches, settlers moving home at a harvest, derived territory, and flow fields rebuilt when growth lays a blocking structure: ADR 0023 (`adr/0023-let-villages-build-found-and-raid-as-they-press-their-caps.md`), which supersedes ADR 0013's rule that footprints block only from the world's creation.
- Buildable, rough and blocked cells, and one test of where something may be built: ADR 0024 (`adr/0024-build-only-on-terrain-whose-kind-bears-buildings.md`), which supersedes ADR 0023's laying clear of an impassable cell.
- A village's clearings extend ADR 0023's spacing as template data, counted as a structure's footprint is, so growth builds around a seat's court rather than into it (issue #120); ADR 0023's rule stands as stated.
- Village caps from housing, food and storage, the daily feeding, hunger, migration and starvation, births slowed by headroom, and fields as structures that block nothing: ADR 0022 (`adr/0022-cap-each-village-by-its-housing-food-and-storage.md`), which supersedes ADR 0013's rule that every footprint blocks.
- Workshops as per-kind capacities and qualities on `Holding`, an edge on the strike rule and daily healing while their capacity covers a village's people, and arms and tends among the holdings growth's scarcity rule compares, rather than shorter cooldowns, healing as a skill, or a trigger of its own: ADR 0029 (`adr/0029-let-villages-build-workshops-that-arm-and-tend-their-people.md`).
- Sects as data with membership on the person and everything else derived, seats as villages, admission and leaving, the cap from a seat's pupils, falling and manuals, and the election by elders' favour: ADR 0026 (`adr/0026-keep-sect-membership-on-the-person-and-seat-each-sect-in-a-village.md`).

- Orders as the one shape of every army's intent, and the player's body as an army of one: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`), whose record of no driver ADR 0038 supersedes.

## Open questions

- **Whether a soul widens cultivation.** A soul's past lives widen its talent by discipline, and not its cultivation, so the realms past beyond peak are reached by no one under the region's data.
- **Qi deviation.** Forcing an art of another nature or above one's realm carries no risk (`docs/concept.md` §Qi and martial arts lists it as proposed).
- **News of a war.** No event records a declaration, so no rumour carries it, and the HUD shows it only in the faction window.
- **Standing feeding relations.** Issue #175's standing of villages, sects and factions toward one another will be another input to `relation` and `hostile`, between a declaration and the stances, leaving declarations a leader's order alone (ADR 0038).
- **Where a soul goes with no village to be born in.** A soul whose body dies while no village has a living resident is not born again.

- **When a settlement fails.** A founded village that raids burn back before its settlers raise it keeps them, and keeps its mother village from founding again, for as long as they live.

- **Whether rubble should free its cells.** Rubble blocks as an intact structure does. Freeing it would mean rebuilding the flow fields that cross it when a structure falls, on the tick it falls.
- **Whether two people on the move can block each other.** A person on the move gives way to another on the move as to anyone, so two at peace marching head-on into each other hold each other up. Two on crossing diagonals that meet side by side do so too, on `main` as now. Foes hold before each other on purpose (ADR 0037).
- **Whether people closing on an objective should keep apart before they stand.** People closing on one objective from either side count one another as behind and come within half a cell before they stand and spread out. Walking straight from one side, a column never did; a column marching more than ten cells short of its objective keeps nearly the separation either way.
- **How the world is simulated at the 512-cell scale.** Four ADRs, proposed, decide it; nothing above changes until the pull requests that build them land:
  - ADR 0042 (`adr/0042-simulate-the-world-in-three-tiers-by-distance-from-the-steered.md`): 32-cell areas in live, stepped and far tiers by distance from the people a driver steers, with hysteresis, a live cap, the same rates in every tier, a far strike rule by expected damage on individuals in roster order, daily rules untiered, and full detail everywhere for a world with no steered army or no tiers. It would supersede the "no levels of detail" above.
  - ADR 0043 (`adr/0043-find-paths-over-a-coarse-graph-of-areas-and-fields-within-them.md`): a coarse graph of areas' components and entrances, paths by A* over it, and fields over an area and a margin that keep ADR 0037's straightest step toward the objective, in place of a flow field per army over the whole map; reachability read from the graph.
  - ADR 0044 (`adr/0044-encode-the-map-in-runs-and-save-the-tiers-state-with-the-world.md`): the map saved in runs, the dead saved without the living's state, the tiers' state saved and hashed, and the save version raised.
  - ADR 0045 (`adr/0045-set-the-population-bound-by-measuring-a-central-plains-scenario.md`): `MAX_PEOPLE` set by measuring a Central Plains scenario on PC from 2,000 to 5,000; 500 until then.
- **Who sells, once people have trades.** `sells` reads a person's best discipline. When issue #170 gives people trades, the trade replaces it there, in that one function (ADR 0040).
- **Silver taken by force.** Issue #175's deeds may let extortion move silver. Silver stays a plain count on the person so a deed can take it.
- **Whether full health is the person's own.** A village's healing restores anyone up to `newborn_health`, the same for every person. A per-person maximum waits for a rule that needs one.
