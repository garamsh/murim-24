# Simulation

The simulation core: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- The simulation core is a C++ library in `core/`, with no engine dependency.
- A world is created from a seed, a map of buildable, rough and blocked cells, armies, structures, rules, villages, each faction's leadership and its rules of life. The rules are data: the separation people keep, the hostility between factions, the floors of the structure stages, and the ticks in a day. It advances one `tick()` at a time.
- Every NPC is a person: a position, health, a cooldown and a home village or none, the people it married and the children born to it, and what its birth gave it: the tick it was born, its family, its parents, its sex, its given name, its talent and attainment per discipline, a lifespan, and a soul or none. A person's identity is its index among the world's people, kept for as long as the world runs, through every army it serves in and after it dies. A world holds at most `MAX_PEOPLE`, 500, living people. That is a hard upper bound, not a target.
- An army is a roster of the living people serving in it, never a count, with an objective, a faction and skills. Every living person stands on exactly one roster. A person who dies leaves its roster but keeps its record, so the dead cost no work on a tick and stay out of the spatial index.
- `conscript()` moves one living person from its roster to another army's. `disband()` returns every member with a home to that village's residents; members without one stay. Neither creates nor removes a person, so the people on rosters are conserved.
- Every army follows an order: an objective cell, and the skills, by index among its own, it strikes with. An army created from data follows its objective with all its skills. `order()` gives an army another order, which it follows from the next tick. That is the port through which a driver steers a body, whether the driver is Godot input or a test, and the same type and resolution serve an NPC and the player alike. Nothing records who steers an army.
- The player's body is an army of one person, born into a village (`world-generation.md`).
- A faction may have a leader, a person, and a succession rule, as data. The only rule is the duel; a faction without one keeps its leader. An order may name a faction to challenge, the same order for the player's body as for an NPC:
  - The challenger is the ordered army's lead. A duel taking members only refuses a challenger from an army of another faction, and a faction with no duel or no leader refuses every challenge.
  - The challenger and the leader become hostile to each other alone and fight by the one skill rule until one has no more than the duel's yield health left. The leader keeps the lead if both fall together.
  - A challenger who wins leads the faction. If the duel says so, every faction at peace with it turns hostile to it, a change to the hostility the world holds.
  - `challenge_refusal` returns why an army's lead may not challenge a faction's leader, a `ChallengeRefusal`, or `NONE` when it may. `order` refuses a challenge exactly when it names a reason, so the rule is written once. `duel` reads the duel being fought for a faction's lead: the challenger and the leader.
- Life is data, `Life`, given with the world. One function bears every person, the player's body and every NPC alike, at creation and on rebirth:
  - talent is a growth and a limit per discipline, each drawn uniformly over a mean plus or minus a spread; a lifespan in days is drawn the same way. A world given no `Life` draws no talent and ages no one
  - a strike that hits anyone or anything practises the striker's best discipline, its highest limit: its attainment there grows by its growth, up to its limit
  - a person dies, as a wound kills, once its age in days passes its lifespan plus `days_per_attainment` days for each point of attainment
- Everyone the world starts with has no parents. A village's first two residents of each of its households, `households` in `VillageSpec`, are born a man and a woman, married to each other, each heading a family of their own; everyone else the world starts with belongs to the family of the army it was created in. A village may name no more households than its residents pair into. A family branches only when one of its people founds a village (below). It was born `founding_age` days, drawn from `Life`, before the world began, so a birth tick is signed.
- Kinship is data, `Kinship` in `Life`, checked once a day; ages are in days and chances per mille a year, rolled from the seed:
  - a living person of marrying age, with a home and no living spouse, marries with the marriage chance a partner drawn among those free to marry, of the other sex and another family, whose home is the same village or one whose centre lies within the neighbourhood. Both record the marriage, in order
  - a wife of bearing age whose husband lives bears a child with the birth chance, no sooner than the spacing after her last, through the one birth function, into her husband's family, or hers if the data says so, at that parent's side, home and army
  - where the data balances sexes, the child takes the sex fewer among the living unmarried whose home is its village and who are not past marrying age, children included; otherwise, or when they are as many, its sex is drawn at even odds
  - no child is born while MAX_PEOPLE live
- Every person bears a name: its family's surname, drawn by the seed for each family as the world begins, and a given name for its sex, drawn at birth, each in Korean and romanised, from `Names` in `Life`. `name()` reads it.
- A soul is the history a person may carry from body to body: the lives it has lived and the disciplines it practised. `ensoul()` gives a living person one. The birth rule reads only that history, empty for a person without a soul:
  - a discipline practised in a past life draws its limit `discipline_widening / lives` further down and up, and every discipline `upper_widening / lives` further up, so the widening shrinks as lives repeat
  - a person carrying a soul who dies is born again on the next tick, in a village drawn by the seed among those with a living resident: into its first resident's family, with its first two residents as parents and that village as home, with a newborn's health. It serves in the army its body served in, which takes the residents' faction and skills and is bound for the birthplace. With no such village the soul is not born again
- The calendar is the core's: a day is `ticks_per_day` ticks, world data in `Rules` that is 1,800 in play, a season 30 days and a year 4 seasons. `date()` derives the year, the season and the day from the tick count.
- Every person moves and strikes on every tick; there are no levels of detail. At 500 people the whole world costs less than the tick budget (below).
- People move toward their order's objective along their army's flow field, rebuilt when an order changes the objective. A step enters any cell the flow field reaches and never a blocked one. An objective must be reachable from where each member stands, when the army is created and when it is given an order.
- People keep the separation, about a cell, from neighbours found through a spatial hash rebuilt each tick:
  - a person steps away from anyone closer than the separation, the harder the closer, and otherwise along its pull toward the objective
  - a person feels no pull within its arrival ring, as many cells from the objective as the square root of its rank in the army, so an arrived army spreads out to the separation
  - a person within two cells of its ring counts as standing still. A person on the move does not give way to people standing still, and they step aside for it, across the way it is going, so a standing crowd parts for someone walking out of it
  - a person on the move does not give way to anyone behind it, the side its pull points away from, so a band queues through a gap one cell wide while each of its people keeps the separation from the one ahead
  - positions are read as the tick began, so the order people move in decides nothing
- A query visits the people it finds in place, with no result list to allocate or sort; every rule that reads it either adds up what it finds or breaks ties by index, so the order of the visit decides nothing.
- A skill is data: range, radius, damage to people, damage to structures and cooldown. One rule resolves every skill, for every person alike, the player's body included:
  - a person whose cooldown has passed draws one of its order's skills by the seed
  - it aims at the nearest living hostile person in range; a skill of range 0 reaches only its user, so it lands where the user stands, with or without anyone hostile near
  - every hostile person in the disc around that point takes the damage once, raised by the edge of the user's home (below), and every structure whose footprint the disc reaches takes the structure damage once
  - damage to structures is signed: a negative amount restores durability, up to the intact stage's floor, and never lowers a structure above that floor. Rebuilding is this rule, not a second one
  - damage to people lands after everyone has struck, and a person whose health reaches 0 dies
- A structure is a footprint of width x height cells from its top-left cell, and a durability. One cell is the 1 x 1 footprint, with no rule of its own. Structures are found by area through a spatial hash of their footprints' centres, queried as far beyond a disc as the widest footprint reaches, and a disc hits a structure when the footprint's nearest point lies within it. Its stage — intact, damaged, destroyed or rubble — follows from durability by the floors in the rules.
- A village is data: its structures and the army living in it, its residents, whose members make it their home. Its owner is its residents' faction. While its residents have a living member, it takes damage only from factions hostile to its owner, and repair only from the others. Once none lives, it takes either, and the first army to restore one of its structures becomes its residents, and its members' home, so the owner becomes that army's faction.
- A village holds food, and what each of its structures holds is data, `Holding`: the people it houses, the food it stores, and the food it yields each harvest, which makes it a field. With husbandry, `Husbandry` in `Life`, once a day:
  - on the first day of winter the village adds its intact fields' harvest to its stock, which never holds more than its storage; it starts with its storage full
  - it feeds everyone whose home it is the consumption a day from its stock, or, when it cannot feed them all, none of them
  - a person unfed for the hunger days moves, by the seed, to a village within the migration reach with room under its cap and food in store, joining its residents, or dies
- A village's cap is derived on each read and never stored, in people: the least of the housing of its structures still standing, intact or damaged, the yearly harvest of its intact fields over a person's yearly consumption, and the storage of its standing structures over the same. Without husbandry it is MAX_PEOPLE. A birth's chance is scaled by the headroom of the child's village, (cap - population) / cap, so births slow near the cap and stop at it.
- A workshop is a structure whose `Holding` also gives the people it arms or tends and what it does for them: an `edge` per mille on body damage, or `healing`, health a day. A village's arms and tends are the growth's `self_reliance`, the people it serves with no workshop, plus the arms, or tends, of its standing structures. While they cover the people whose home it is, those people strike with the greatest edge among its standing structures, a strike's body damage multiplied by (1,000 + edge) / 1,000, and once a day recover the greatest healing among them, up to `newborn_health`. A ruined workshop does neither. All of it is derived on read.
- Tests show, each with a control: residents striking half again as hard beside a smithy intact or damaged, and as before once it is destroyed or arms fewer than them; the wounded healing a day's healing beside an apothecary intact or damaged, up to a newborn's health and never lowering anyone above it, and not at all once it is destroyed or tends fewer than them; and a village with self-reliance below its housing laying a smithy, then an apothecary, where one with self-reliance equal to it lays a house.
- Villages grow by one rule, `Growth` in `Life`, checked every `interval` days, with no branch per faction. A village with living residents presses against its cap once the people whose home it is reach `pressure` per mille of it. It then takes the first expansion of its owner's stance, per-faction data, that it can:
  - build: while every structure of its own is intact, it lays the plan, among those it builds, that most raises its scarcest holding counted in people: housing, food, storage, arms and tends, in that order among equals. The plan is laid as rubble, on the free buildable cells within `vicinity` of its centre nearest it, a cell clear of any structure or unbuildable cell. Its residents raise it by the repair rule, and it houses, stores, yields, arms or tends once it stands. A village whose other holdings exceed its self-reliance thus builds a smithy, then an apothecary; by default self-reliance is as many as a `std::uint32_t` holds, and growth builds no workshop
  - found: with more residents than its settlers and none still settling, it lays the settlement's layout as rubble on free buildable land drawn by the seed. The land's centre lies within `reach` of its own, `spacing` cells clear of any structure, and held by no other faction. The settlers march there as the new village's residents: a founder, the first resident of the lineage's sex who leads no faction, its living spouse and unmarried children, then others in roster order. The founder and its children take a new family, a branch bearing its surname. The layout makes a village of the growth's `settlement_kind`, and `settlement()` reads the land a village was founded on: that kind, the settlement area and its corner; nothing for a village the world was created with
  - raid: with more residents than its raiders and no party out, it sends the first raiders who lead no faction against the nearest village of a faction it is hostile to, with living residents, within `reach`. The village keeps that army as its party and reuses it. The raid ends once the party has no one left, the target has no living resident, or its owner is no longer one the party is hostile to, and its members return to their homes. A party that becomes another village's residents by restoring a structure there is no longer the village's
- Settlers keep their old home until a harvest finds their new village's cap holding all its living residents, which then makes it their home.
- A cell lies in the territory of the owner of the nearest village with living residents whose centre lies within `claim` cells, derived on each read. `territory()` reads it.
- Growth lays a structure only where it covers no army's objective and no living person, and only if every person can still reach its army's objective and the settlers their new centre. Laying one that blocks rebuilds every army's flow field. `plan_of` reads the plan a structure was laid from, and `army_count`, `structure_count` and `village_count` count what growth added.
- A field shows its season, sown in spring, growing in summer, ripe in autumn and fallow in winter, and is burnt whenever it is below its intact stage. A torch burns it by the one strike rule, and mending restores it.
- A village's state is derived on each read and never stored:
  - ruins while every structure is rubble
  - otherwise raided while a living person of a faction hostile to its owner could damage one of its structures from where it stands: a skill that damages structures, its range and radius away
  - otherwise prosperous while every structure is intact, and rebuilding while one is not
- A village records who ruined it: the army, and its faction, whose strike brought its last standing structure to rubble. The record outlives rebuilding and is part of the hash.
- A structure's footprint blocks movement for as long as the world runs, rubble included, unless its kind does not block, as a field does not. Flow fields change only when growth lays a structure that blocks. Footprints must lie on passable cells, cover no other structure, and leave every person's start uncovered.
- A cell is buildable, rough or blocked: bodies cross a rough cell, and nothing is built on one. `Map::buildable` is the one test of where something may be built, which growth reads here and the region's generation reads for its villages. A test shows a house laid off rough cells that open ground would have put it on.
- A sect is data given with the world, `SectSpec`: a name, a faction and a seat, a village by index, with founders who join it at a starting attainment in their discipline of highest talent limit. Membership is the person's `sect`, and everything else about a sect is derived on read:
  - its members are its living people
  - its elders are the `elder_share` per mille of its members of the highest best attainment, from its faction's `Admission`, the lower index among equals, and at least one while it stands; its master is the elder of the highest, the lower index among equals
  - its seat is held while the village is not in ruins and its owner is the sect's faction, and its cap is then the `pupils` of the seat's standing structures, and 0 otherwise
  - it stands while it has a living member
- Once a day, after kinship, sects are kept:
  - a member serving in an army of another faction leaves its sect
  - each manual a fallen sect holds scatters, with `Manuals`' chance, to a passable cell drawn by the seed within its reach of the seat's centre, and is otherwise lost; a manual's place is stored, a sect, a cell or lost
  - a living person of no sect, with a home, of a faction with an `Admission`, old enough, with a talent limit and an attainment at its thresholds in one discipline, whose home lies within its reach of a sect of the faction with its seat held, a living elder and room under its cap, joins one such sect drawn by the seed, with the admission's chance a year
- A faction with an `Election` whose leader is dead or missing, or on a day its term divides, is led by the master of its sects with the most elders, each elder favouring its own sect's master, ties to the higher best attainment and then the lower index; with no master it keeps its leader. It has no duel, so every challenge on it is refused. `elects` reads whether a faction has an election.
- `sect_count`, `sect_of`, `sect_name`, `sect_faction`, `sect_seat`, `seat_held`, `sect_members`, `sect_elders`, `sect_master`, `sect_cap`, `sect_stands` and `manuals` read sects and manuals.
- Tests show, each with a control: admission of a faction's people within reach up to the cap, and of no one below a threshold, beyond reach or of another faction; a lost seat, ruined or held by another faction, capping its sect at 0 and keeping its members; a member conscripted into another faction's army leaving, and one into its own staying; elders as the share of members of the highest attainment, at least one, and the next elder becoming master when the master leaves; a fallen sect's manual scattering within reach, to the same cell for the same seed, or lost, and a fallen sect admitting no one; and an election making the master leader when no one leads, and `elects` reading a faction with an election as electing and the same faction without one as not.
- All simulation state is integers: positions are fixed-point at 1/256 of a cell.
- A world exposes a hash of its whole state. Determinism is tested by comparing hashes: the same seed gives the same hash after 10,000 ticks with armies moving, fighting, losing people and breaking structures. A test pins that hash, so it must be the same on every compiler and platform; it folds each person's sect, the sects, the manuals, and each faction's admission and election. The hash covers every person, living or dead, with what its birth gave it, the souls, each army's roster and order, and the villages, and a log of orders, each with the tick it was given before, replays to the same hash.
- The world ticks 30 times a second, so a rate given per second, such as skills, becomes work per tick.
- The tick budget is a p99 tick time of at most 4 ms on the development Mac (Apple Silicon), on the M1 benchmark scenario in `core/m1_scenario.cpp`, the worst case: MAX_PEOPLE people, every one in one battle of two lines with a barricade between them, and two caster armies breaking it. On 2026-10-07 it measured a tick p99 of 0.618 ms. `make bench` measures it. Timing depends on the machine, so `make ci` does not run it.
- `make sweep` lives the generated region of each seed in a range, 0 to 49 by default, for a number of years, 100 by default, with days of five ticks. Per seed it prints the households each faction starts with, the living and the peopled villages at the start and the end, the peak, and whether every faction kept a peopled village at the end of every year. It tunes data and gates nothing, so `make ci` does not run it.
- The core is built, tested, linted and formatted with the toolchain ADR 0003 chose. Lint parses it on every host against one pinned Ubuntu 24.04 sysroot, unpacked by `lint-sysroot.sh`, so local and CI lint read the same standard library.
- `age_in_years` reads a person's age in whole years of the calendar, and `faction_of` the faction of the army a person serves in, none once dead. Both read what the world holds and change nothing.

## Rationale

- The core is C++ and loads no engine, so its rules run headless: ADR 0002 (`adr/0002-build-on-godot-with-a-cpp-simulation-core.md`).
- The build, test framework, formatter and linter, and how CI installs them: ADR 0003 (`adr/0003-build-the-core-with-cmake-googletest-and-llvm-tools.md`).
- Lint against one pinned Linux sysroot, because clang-tidy's findings depend on the standard library it parses: ADR 0016 (`adr/0016-lint-against-one-pinned-linux-sysroot-on-every-host.md`).
- A person on the move ignoring people behind it, so a crowd passes a gap: ADR 0020 (`adr/0020-let-a-person-on-the-move-ignore-people-behind-it.md`), which supersedes ADR 0017's rule that people on the move give way to one another.
- Every NPC a person with an identity, armies as rosters, conscription and disbanding, no levels of detail, the population bound, and the movement rule: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`), which supersedes ADRs 0005 and 0007 and ADR 0006's multiplicities.
- Flow fields, the spatial hash, and fixed-point numbers for determinism across builds: ADR 0005 (`adr/0005-simulate-armies-at-three-levels-of-detail-in-fixed-point.md`), whose tiers and aggregates ADR 0017 removes.
- Skills as data, the single rule that resolves them, and structures and their stages: ADR 0006 (`adr/0006-resolve-every-skill-through-one-rule-scaled-by-count.md`), whose scaling by count ADR 0017 removes.
- Villages, their derived state, repair as a signed structure effect, owners following residents, and the recorded destroyer: ADR 0015 (`adr/0015-run-the-village-life-cycle-from-structures-and-residents.md`).
- Footprints, the hit rule they share, footprints that block in every stage, and objectives that must be reachable: ADR 0013 (`adr/0013-give-structures-footprints-that-block-movement-in-every-stage.md`).
- Leaders as people, the duel as faction data fought by the one skill rule, and challenges through the order port: ADR 0018 (`adr/0018-change-a-factions-leader-by-duel-as-faction-data.md`).
- Growth by one rule of stances and pressure, building and founding by laying rubble raised by repair, family branches, settlers moving home at a harvest, derived territory, and flow fields rebuilt when growth lays a blocking structure: ADR 0023 (`adr/0023-let-villages-build-found-and-raid-as-they-press-their-caps.md`), which supersedes ADR 0013's rule that footprints block only from the world's creation.
- Buildable, rough and blocked cells, and one test of where something may be built: ADR 0024 (`adr/0024-build-only-on-terrain-whose-kind-bears-buildings.md`), which supersedes ADR 0023's laying clear of an impassable cell.
- Village caps from housing, food and storage, the daily feeding, hunger, migration and starvation, births slowed by headroom, and fields as structures that block nothing: ADR 0022 (`adr/0022-cap-each-village-by-its-housing-food-and-storage.md`), which supersedes ADR 0013's rule that every footprint blocks.
- Workshops as per-kind capacities and qualities on `Holding`, an edge on the strike rule and daily healing while their capacity covers a village's people, and arms and tends among the holdings growth's scarcity rule compares, rather than shorter cooldowns, healing as a skill, or a trigger of its own: ADR 0029 (`adr/0029-let-villages-build-workshops-that-arm-and-tend-their-people.md`).
- Sects as data with membership on the person and everything else derived, seats as villages, admission and leaving, the cap from a seat's pupils, falling and manuals, and the election by elders' favour: ADR 0026 (`adr/0026-keep-sect-membership-on-the-person-and-seat-each-sect-in-a-village.md`).
- Elders as a share of a sect's members by attainment, at least one, since members' attainment rises to their talent limit and no threshold kept elders a minority: ADR 0028 (`adr/0028-make-a-sects-elders-the-share-of-its-strongest-members.md`), which supersedes ADR 0026's elders by an attainment threshold.
- Households of married couples, each heading a family of its own, as a village's founders: ADR 0025 (`adr/0025-start-each-village-from-households-of-married-couples.md`), which supersedes ADR 0021's one family per starting army for the families of households.
- A child of the sex its village's unmarried lack, so a faction's line does not end on a run of one sex, and the alternatives measured against it: ADR 0027 (`adr/0027-bear-each-child-of-the-sex-its-village-lacks.md`), which supersedes ADR 0021's even-odds sex for those children.
- Marriage, children and names as data under one rule for everyone, births capped by the living bound, founding ages, and the day's ticks as world data: ADR 0021 (`adr/0021-let-people-marry-have-children-and-bear-family-names.md`), which supersedes ADR 0019's day fixed in the core.
- Talent and lifespan drawn at birth by one rule, ageing, souls born again into a family of the same world with a widened draw, and the calendar: ADR 0019 (`adr/0019-draw-talent-and-lifespan-at-birth-and-bear-a-soul-again.md`).
- Orders as the one shape of every army's intent, and the player's body as an army of one: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).

## Open questions

- **Which discipline a strike practises.** Skills carry no discipline, so a strike practises the striker's best one. Martial arts (`docs/concept.md` §Martial arts) are to give each technique its discipline.
- **Where a soul goes with no village to be born in.** A soul whose body dies while no village has a living resident is not born again.

- **When a settlement fails.** A founded village that raids burn back before its settlers raise it keeps them, and keeps its mother village from founding again, for as long as they live.

- **Whether rubble should free its cells.** Rubble blocks as an intact structure does. Freeing it would mean rebuilding the flow fields that cross it when a structure falls, on the tick it falls.
- **Whether two people on the move can block each other.** A person on the move gives way to another on the move as to anyone, so two marching head-on into each other may hold each other up. No test or scenario covers it yet.
- **Whether full health is the person's own.** A village's healing restores anyone up to `newborn_health`, the same for every person. A per-person maximum waits for a rule that needs one.
