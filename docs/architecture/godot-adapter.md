# Godot adapter

The GDExtension adapter through which Godot drives the simulation core: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- The adapter is a GDExtension library built from `extension/`, linking `core/` unchanged. `murim.gdextension` at the repository root loads it into the Godot project there.
- `make build` builds the library for the host. `make android` builds it for Android arm64 through the NDK's CMake toolchain file, with the same `extension/CMakeLists.txt`, and exports a debug APK whose launch runs `world_view/frame_bench.gd`.
- It registers one class, `WorldAdapter`, which creates a core world, ticks it, and reads back its hash, each army's faction and its members' health when created, together, the positions and health of its living members, one person each, each army's skills, and each structure's cell, footprint, kind and stage. GDScript cannot construct it; `WorldAdapter.create`, `WorldAdapter.create_m1` and `WorldAdapter.create_region` are the only ways to get one.
- `create_m1` builds the core's M1 benchmark scenario (`core/m1_scenario.cpp`), the same one the tick benchmark runs; `m1_centre` gives the point its battle is fought around.
- `create_region` builds the region the core generates from a seed, with each faction's leadership, and one person born into a village, the first unless another is named, as the last army. It keeps what the view draws of the region: each cell's terrain kind and owning faction, the kinds' and factions' names, and the villages, read back by `terrain`, `terrain_names`, `territory`, `faction_names` and `villages`, each village with the name of its template. Its structures carry their kinds, and so do the M1 scenario's, which are barrels. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no region, and these return empty.
- `order` passes an order to the core: an army, an objective cell as a `Vector2i`, skill indices as a `PackedInt64Array`, and optionally a faction whose leader its lead challenges. An order the core refuses makes it report the refusal with `push_error` and return false. `map_rows` and `skill_count` read back what a driver needs to aim one.
- Leadership is read back as the core holds it:
  - `leader` gives each faction's leading person, and `duel` the two people fighting for a faction's lead.
  - `challenge_refusal` gives the core's verdict on a challenge before it is made, one of the `CHALLENGE_*` constants.
  - `hostile`, `roster`, `person_position` and `person_health` read what a duel changes and who fights it.
- Positions cross in sub-cell units as `Vector2`s, exact while a coordinate stays within 2^24. The tick rate, the cell size and the structure stages cross as the class's integer constants, so GDScript repeats none of them.
- `create` takes what the core's constructor takes, as Godot types: a seed, map rows as a `PackedStringArray`, and the armies (with their skills), structures, rules and, optionally, each faction's leadership as `Dictionary`s keyed by the core's field names, with cells as `Vector2i`. Converting them to the core's types, and the hash back to a signed int, is the adapter's work.
- Input the core refuses, or that is missing, of the wrong type or outside the core's integer types, makes `create` report it with `push_error` and return null. No exception crosses into Godot.
- It holds no game rule: every call is passed to the core, which decides what is valid.
- `world_art/world_art.tscn` draws a world in the approved art, and both scenes below use it. It holds no game rule.
  - Terrain is laid as `docs/convention/stack-godot.md` §Terrain layers sets out. A world without a region draws its passable cells as plain and its blocked cells as mountain.
  - Territory borders are a dotted line in each owner's accent. Landforms are `Sprite2D`s.
  - Each structure is a `Sprite2D` over its footprint, showing its stage's frame of its kind's art: house, hall, well, barrels or fence. A structure from `create` has no kind and is not drawn.
  - Each village's decorations come from `world_art/villages.json` by its template's name: paving, road and fields as ground tiles on a layer above the terrain, and a banner in its owner's colours standing with everything else. They never block or break.
  - Bodies are drawn only on screen, through one `MultiMesh` per row of cells, from one texture holding every faction's sprites. Every body is one person, drawn as its faction's sprite, walking and facing the way it moved since the last tick.
  - The rows, the structures and the landforms are sorted together by the bottom edge of what they stand on. The steered person carries the player's ring and chevron.
- `world_view/world_view.tscn` shows the M1 scenario through `world_art.tscn`, starting its camera at `m1_centre`, and ticks at the core's tick rate whatever the frame rate. `make bench` renders it.
- The view renders at 480 x 270 and scales to the window by whole multiples, drawing textures without filtering.
- The world is presented in a three-quarter top-down view, one core cell to one 16-pixel tile, with combat in real time on the core's tick. Ground and roofs are seen from above; facades and standing bodies are drawn upright and sorted by the bottom edge of their footprint. A structure is drawn over the footprint of width x height cells the core gives it, which `structure_footprint` reads back; `create` takes each army's members as cells and health, and no footprint, so every structure it makes is 1 x 1. Art is pixel art on that grid, from the Aseprite sources under `art/`, made to `docs/art-direction.md`. UI text is Korean first, with English supported.
- `play/play.tscn`, the project's main scene, steers the person `create_region` bears. A left click orders it toward the cell clicked, keys 1 to 9 order it to strike with that skill, and 0 with all its skills; an order keeps whatever part the input does not change. C has it challenge its faction's leader, marching on the leader, when `challenge_refusal` names no reason; either way the HUD shows the verdict. Its camera follows the person. It draws the world through `world_art.tscn` and the HUD through `hud/hud.tscn`.
- `hud/hud.tscn` shows the player's portrait and faction; health as a percentage of its health when born over its bar, and inner energy as a dash over an empty bar; a map of the region around the player; a rumour log; its faction's leader, with C to challenge; while a duel for the lead is fought, both duellists' health against what each had when it began, and when it ends whether the lead changed hands; the verdict on the player's challenge, or why it was refused; and the technique bar, one slot per skill with its number key and an icon of its shape, `burst` when its radius reaches past its target and `strike` otherwise. It draws in Galmuri9. Its words come from `hud/hud.csv` through Godot's translations: Korean at start, and F2 switches between Korean and English.
- A headless Godot test shows that a world of two hostile armies with skills, and structures, driven through the adapter, gives the core's hash, and its people's positions and health, for the same scenario and tick count, against `world_hash`, a program that runs the core with no Godot; and that invalid scenarios return null while a valid one does not. It also shows that a person ordered through the adapter leaves home and strikes a hostile camp down, while one left without an order does neither. A second test shows `play.tscn` turning a click and keys into orders: a click on a passable cell moves the person toward it, and a click on a blocked cell and a key for a skill the person lacks are refused. A third shows `world_art.tscn` laying the plain on every cell, and each dual-grid tile with the corner mask of its four cells. It also checks that every body on screen and none off it is drawn, that each structure shows its stage's frame of its kind's art, and that each village's ground decorations cover the cells its template's data names. A fourth shows the HUD's words in Korean, then in English after a switch, its health and inner-energy values, and an icon for each skill. A fifth drives a challenge through the adapter: a member's challenge starts a duel the adapter reads, and a win makes the challenger leader and turns the Cult's ally hostile; an outsider is refused for not being a member, and a lost duel leaves the leader. `play_test` also shows C refused, with its reason on the HUD, from an Orthodox village, and accepted, with the duel shown, from a Cult one.

## Rationale

- Godot is the presentation adapter and the core reaches it through GDExtension: ADR 0002 (`adr/0002-build-on-godot-with-a-cpp-simulation-core.md`).
- The binding release, its pin, the build path, the scenario format, how refusals reach GDScript, the test runner and how CI installs Godot: ADR 0004 (`adr/0004-bind-the-core-to-godot-with-godot-cpp-through-cmake.md`).
- Every body one person, read and drawn one by one, and no focus to set: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`).
- Steering through the orders every army follows: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).
- The three-quarter top-down view, structure footprints, real-time combat, pixel art on a 16-pixel tile grid and Korean-first UI text, and the alternatives rejected: ADR 0012 (`adr/0012-present-the-world-in-a-three-quarter-top-down-view.md`), which supersedes ADR 0008 (`adr/0008-present-the-world-top-down-in-real-time-as-pixel-art.md`).

## Open questions

- **Inner energy and the calendar.** The core has neither. The HUD's inner-energy bar stands empty with a dash for its value, and its season and year are a fixed placeholder, until the core holds them.
- **Duel pacing.** In the generated region every person has 100,000 health and the Cult's fighting skill does 1 damage every 15 ticks, so a duel to the death runs about 1.5 million ticks; it starts and shows in play but does not resolve in a session. Pacing is core data.
- **Rumours.** The HUD's rumour log shows a placeholder line until the core spreads rumours (`docs/concept.md` §Reputation and rumors).

- **Which platforms the library is built for.** It is built for the host, macOS arm64 and Linux x86_64 so far, and for Android arm64; iOS exports are not built.
