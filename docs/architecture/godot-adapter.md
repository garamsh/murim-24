# Godot adapter

The GDExtension adapter through which Godot drives the simulation core: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- The adapter is a GDExtension library built from `extension/`, linking `core/` unchanged. `murim.gdextension` at the repository root loads it into the Godot project there.
- `make build` builds the library for the host. `make android` builds it for Android arm64 through the NDK's CMake toolchain file, with the same `extension/CMakeLists.txt`, and exports two debug APKs from the same library: the `Android` preset's, which opens the game on the title screen, and the `Android bench` preset's, whose launch runs `world_view/frame_bench.gd`. `make export` builds release libraries for macOS arm64 and x86_64 in one universal library, and for Windows x86_64 by cross-building with a pinned llvm-mingw, and exports a release build for each that opens on the title screen.
- It registers one class, `WorldAdapter`, which creates a core world, ticks it, and reads back its hash, its date, each army's members' health when created, together, its faction and skills now, the positions and health of its living members, one person each, and each structure's cell, footprint, kind and stage. GDScript cannot construct it; `WorldAdapter.create`, `WorldAdapter.create_m1`, `WorldAdapter.create_region` and `WorldAdapter.load` are the only ways to get one.
- `create_m1` builds the core's M1 benchmark scenario (`core/m1_scenario.cpp`), the same one the tick benchmark runs; `m1_centre` gives the point its battle is fought around.
- `create_region` builds the region the core generates from a seed, with its villages, each faction's leadership and its rules of life, and one person born into a village, the first unless another is named, as the last army. Its days last the core's 1,800 ticks unless another length is given, which `Rules::ticks_per_day` takes; a day of no ticks is refused with null. That person carries a soul, so when it dies it is born again into the same army, which it marks steered through the core's `steer`, so the rules that decide for people on their own decide nothing for it; the mark is saved with the world. `steer` and `steered` mark and read any army. It keeps what the view draws of the region: each cell's terrain kind, the kinds' and factions' names, and the villages, read back by `terrain`, `terrain_names`, `faction_names` and `villages`, each village with the name of its template and its owner now. `villages` lists the region's villages, then those founded as the world runs, each with the kind, corner and area the core's `settlement` gives and named as the region numbers its own. `territory` reads each cell's faction as the core derives it now, or -1 for a cell no village claims. Its structures carry their kinds, and so do the M1 scenario's, which are barrels. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no region, and these return empty.
- `save` writes the world to a slot through the core's save port, served by `FileSaves`, which keeps slot `n` as `slot_<n>.sav` in a directory: `user://saves`, which `saves_directory` gives, unless `save`, `load` or `load_refusal` is given another, as the tests give `user://test_saves`; it refuses a world made by `create` or `create_m1`, which has no region, with `push_error` and false. `WorldAdapter.load` reads a slot back: it regenerates what the view draws from the region's seed and the player's birth village, which the save holds, and returns null after `push_error` naming the core's `LoadError`. `load_refusal` gives that `LoadError` as one of the `LOAD_*` constants, `LOAD_NOT_A_SAVE` for a save of a world with no region, or `LOAD_NONE` when the core reads the slot; it decodes the save without regenerating the region. `player_army` gives the army the living person carrying the player's soul serves in, read from the core, so a loaded world finds its player with no field of its own, or -1 while none lives. `region_seed` gives the seed the region was generated from, or -1 for a world with no region.
- `order` passes an order to the core: an army, an objective cell as a `Vector2i`, skill indices as a `PackedInt64Array`, optionally a faction whose leader its lead challenges, and optionally a person its army holds an attack intent on. An order the core refuses makes it report the refusal with `push_error` and return false. `map_rows` and `skill_count` read back what a driver needs to aim one. `attack_refusal` gives the core's verdict on an army taking a person as its intent's target, one of the `ATTACK_*` constants, `target` reads the intent's target or -1, `objective` an army's objective cell, `end_intent` ends an army's intent, holding it where its lead stands, and `foes_in_sight` lists a person's foes within sight, nearest first.
- Leadership is read back as the core holds it:
  - `leader` gives each faction's leading person, `elects` whether the faction elects its leader, and `duel` the two people fighting for a faction's lead.
  - `challenge_refusal` gives the core's verdict on a challenge before it is made, one of the `CHALLENGE_*` constants.
  - `hostile`, `roster`, `person_position` and `person_health` read what a duel changes and who fights it.
  - `relation` gives how one faction stands toward another, one of the `RELATION_*` constants, the core's one read of it; `declaration` the declaration standing between two, a Dictionary of `what`, a `DECLARATION_*` constant, `by`, `tick`, and `year` and `season` as the core's calendar dates that tick, or empty; `declaration_refusal` the core's verdict on a declaration before it is made, one of the `DECLARE_*` constants; and `declare` gives it, reporting a refusal with `push_error` and false.
- Each person's name and kin are read back as the core holds them: `person_name` gives its surname and given name in Korean and romanised, `names` every surname and female and male given name the world's rules of life were given, in Korean and romanised, empty for a world from `create`, and `person_parents`, `person_children`, `person_spouse` (the last it married) and `person_family` its kin; `person_age` its age in whole years of the core's calendar, and `person_faction` the faction of the army it serves in, or -1 for one serving in none, as the dead do. The genealogy panel and the HUD read them.
- Each village's cap, stock and population are read back as the core derives them by `village_cap`, `village_stock` and `village_population`, villages founded as the world runs included, and each field's state by `structure_field_state`, one of the `FIELD_*` constants or -1 for a structure that is no field.
- Sects and manuals are read back as the core holds them: `sect_count`, `person_sect` (-1 for none), `sect_name`, `sect_faction`, `sect_seat`, `sect_seat_held`, `sect_members` and `sect_elders` as person indices, `sect_master` (-1 for none), `sect_cap` and `sect_stands`, and `manuals`, each a Dictionary of its `art` and its `place`, "sect", "cell" or "lost", with its `sect` or `cell`. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no sects or manuals.
- A person applies to a sect through the core: `application_refusal` gives the core's verdict, one of the `APPLICATION_*` constants, and `apply` makes the person a member, or reports the core's refusal with `push_error` and returns false.
- Qi is read back as the core holds it: `person_qi` and `person_qi_capacity`, `person_realm`, the rank, `realms`, each realm's name in Korean and romanised, its qi and its might, `person_nature`, the orientation and polarity of a person's heart method as `ORIENTATION_*` and `POLARITY_*` constants, empty while it knows no art, and `person_skills`, the skills a person may cast by the index `cast` takes, its army's and then the art skills it knows, each with its cost, its ready tick and, for an art skill, its art, its index among the art's skills, rank, name and nature. `arts` gives every art of the world's data, its name in Korean and romanised, its nature and its skills in the order they are learned, each with its name, the rank it needs, its range, radius, cooldown and cost, so the arts panel lists the skills a person has not learned too. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no realms or arts, and its people hold no qi.
- A person casts through the core: `cast` takes a person, a skill by index among `person_skills`, and a target, a `Vector2` point in sub-cell units, an int person or null for itself, and reports the core's refusal, or a target of another type, with `push_error` and false. `cast_refusal` gives the core's verdict first, one of the `CAST_*` constants, or -1 after `push_error` for a target of another type. `aim` gives the hostile person a skill would aim at, favouring a facing given as a `Vector2`, or -1 for none. `skills` gives each skill's range, cooldown and shape, one of the `SHAPE_*` constants, `body_ready` when each body may use one again, and `tick_count` the tick the world has reached, which a ready tick is counted against, for the controls' indicators.
- `rumours` reads the events that reach a person as the core derives them, newest first, each a Dictionary of its `sequence`, `tick`, `cell` and `kind`, "founded", "raided", "ruined", "sect_fell", "leader_changed", "born" or "died", with the records it names. `person_attainment` reads a person's attainment by discipline, and `discipline_names` the disciplines the world's rules of life draw, empty for a world from `create` or `create_m1`.
- Talking is decided by the core: `talk_refusal` gives its verdict on one person talking to another, one of the `TALK_*` constants, `TALK_NONE` when it may, and `offers` what a person offers one who talks to it, as `OFFER_*` constants in order, `OFFER_NEWS` while it lives, then `OFFER_TRADE` while it sells any ware, and none once dead. A region's people talk within 2 cells.
- Trade is decided by the core: `wares` gives the trade's wares, each a Dictionary of its `name`, `price`, `health`, `qi` and `discipline`; `person_silver` and `person_held` what a person holds; `sells` whether a person sells a ware; `buy_refusal` and `consume_refusal` the verdicts, one of the `BUY_*` and `CONSUME_*` constants; and `buy` and `consume` act on them, returning false, after one error, for a refused order.
- `strikes` gives every use of a skill the core recorded on the last tick, in the order people struck, each a Dictionary of the person, the skill's index among its skills, its shape as a `SHAPE_*` constant, its range, radius and body damage, the points it went from and toward and the one its shape reaches as far as in sub-cell units, and the people it hit; the world art draws them. `body_ready` gives the tick from which each of an army's living members may strike again, which moves on each strike, in the order of `body_positions`. `events` gives the events the core keeps from a given sequence on, oldest first, each with its sequence, tick, cell and kind, one of the `EVENT_*` constants: founded, raided, ruined, sect fell, leader changed, born or died, and for a birth or a death the person. The sound scene and the world art read them. `progress` gives the steps of progress the core keeps from a given sequence on, oldest first, each with its sequence, tick, kind, one of the `PROGRESS_*` constants, learned or realm gained, and person; a skill learned also gives its art, the skill's index in the art and how it was learned, one of the `LEARNED_BY_*` constants, comprehension, manual or teaching, and a realm gained its rank. The HUD reads them for its notices.
- `army_count` and `structure_count` count what the world holds now, the armies and structures its villages' growth adds included. A structure growth laid takes its plan's kind in `structure_kind`. `army_strength` gives 0 for an army formed as the world runs.
- Positions cross in sub-cell units as `Vector2`s, exact while a coordinate stays within 2^24. The tick rate, the cell size, an arc's half-width and the structure stages cross as the class's integer constants, so GDScript repeats none of them.
- `create` takes what the core's constructor takes, as Godot types: a seed, map rows as a `PackedStringArray`, and the armies (with their skills, each with a shape as a `SHAPE_*` constant, an area where it is left out), structures, rules and, optionally, each faction's leadership as `Dictionary`s keyed by the core's field names, with cells as `Vector2i`. Converting them to the core's types, and the hash back to a signed int, is the adapter's work.
- Input the core refuses, or that is missing, of the wrong type or outside the core's integer types, makes `create` report it with `push_error` and return null. No exception crosses into Godot.
- It holds no game rule: every call is passed to the core, which decides what is valid.
- `world_art/world_art.tscn` draws a world in the approved art, and both scenes below use it. It holds no game rule.
  - The world is drawn at Albion's angle (ADR 0059): a cell 16 pixels wide and 12 deep, ground on level `n` raised 16 pixels times `n`. `world_art/relief.gd` gives each cell its kind of ground, level, face material and whether it rises as a slope, derived from the core's terrain kinds until the core's map carries levels: water a level down behind a bank, plain and forest flat, hills slopes to levels 1 and 2, and mountains rock in steps of levels 2, 4 and 5. A cliff stands only where the core blocks movement. A world without a region draws its passable cells as plain and its blocked cells as mountain. Every cell-to-pixel and pixel-to-cell turn goes through `to_screen`, `ground_point`, `to_sub` and `cell_at`, raised with the ground, a click picking the ground drawn under it.
  - `world_art/ground.gd` draws the ground off the cell grid with `world_art/ground.gdshader`, as `docs/convention/stack-godot.md` §Ground sets out, coloured from each kind's ramp in `art/terrain/ramps` and its detail from `art/terrain/detail`. `relief.gd` repeats the shader's heights, so bodies stand on what it draws. Every cell a structure stands on, and every paved or road cell, is marked built in the relief. Where the ground round a built cell is flat, the shader draws the ground there without warp or dither, of the cell's own kind and level, so it meets what is built in a straight seam along the cells' sides; on or beside a slope or a drop it draws the ground as any other, so no cliff stands where the core blocks nothing. It bakes every 32-cell area once, from the first frame a renderer can draw them, into a viewport freed after the last. Each area scatters detail and decorative trees by Poisson-disc spacing from a seed its index gives, with a shadow under each tree, none on a village or a structure, and is cut into its ground and a strip for each raised row. Only the areas within 64 pixels of the screen are shown, one more a frame once the first are, and those a further area away are freed. Without a renderer, as under `make test`, nothing is baked.
  - Territory borders are a dotted line in each owner's accent, on the side of each cell a faction holds, drawn from `territory`. Each tick reads it and redraws them, and signals the change, only when it moved or a village was founded.
  - Each structure is a `Sprite2D` over its footprint, showing its stage's frame of its kind's art: house, hall, well, barrels, fence, granary, storehouse, training ground, manual hall, seat gate, smithy or apothecary. Each kind's frame holds its footprint's bottom-left corner 8 pixels in from its left and bottom edges, as `docs/art-direction.md` sets out, and is drawn with that corner on the ground's, raised with it. Each standing structure casts a shadow on the ground past its right and bottom edges, a dithered band that narrows when it is destroyed and goes with rubble, swapped as its stage changes. A training ground is laid over the ground, under everything standing, so the people on it are drawn over it. A structure from `create` has no kind and is not drawn. A structure growth lays is given its sprite on the next refresh, and an army growth forms is read on the next tick.
  - Each village's decorations come from `world_art/villages.json` by its template's name: paving and road as tiles a cell each on a layer over the ground, a seat's paved court and its path to the gate among them, and banners in its owner's colours standing with everything else: a village's banner, and a seat's sect banners. They never block or break. A founded village's are laid on the tick that finds it, as a generated one's are.
  - Each field is tiled from `art/decorations/fields`, a cell to a tile, by the state the core gives it, sown, growing, ripe, fallow or burnt, with its winter tag in winter, re-tiled when its state or the season changes.
  - Bodies are drawn only on screen, their feet on the point the core gives them raised with the ground, each over its shadow, through one `MultiMesh` per row of cells, from one texture holding every faction's sprites. Each row's bounds span the region's width, so a row refilled after the view moves is not culled by where it was first drawn. Every body is one person, drawn as its faction's sprite, walking and facing the way it moved since the last tick, its back shown when it walked away from the viewer, and its strike for 200 ms after a use of its own body damage the core recorded. A body is drawn between where the last two ticks left it, as far between as the frame lies from the last tick toward the next, so it moves on every frame and not only on a tick; the core's positions stay the only ones there are. An army whose roster changed on the last tick has its bodies drawn where that tick left them. The steered person's ring and chevron stand where its body is drawn.
  - The rows of bodies, the structures, the trees and the raised strips of the ground are sorted together by the row they stand on, so whoever stands behind raised ground is drawn behind it.
  - A body whose frame overlaps a structure, a tree or a raised strip sorted after it, read from buckets of 32 pixels rebuilt when the areas shown, the structures or a structure's stage change, is outlined: one `MultiMesh` over everything standing draws its outline frame, through `world_art/outline.gdshader`, only on the pixels where the screen does not show the body itself in the colour its band drew it, flashed as it is struck or not, `world_art/flash.gdshaderinc` deciding the flash for both. `frame_bench` prints how many bodies a frame outlined. The steered person carries the player's ring and chevron.
  - Names are read around a viewer, the steered army in `play.tscn`, and relations toward its faction through `relation`, once a tick for every army. Each body whose army's faction stands hostile or at war is ringed in crimson `d63a2f` outside its ink outline: its band draws each clear pixel of its frame beside a solid one in the colour its instance carries. Each refresh lists the people on screen within 8 cells, `NAME_REACH`, of the viewer's lead, the lead left out, nearest first, with how each stands, for their names.
  - What the fighting did is drawn by `world_art/combat.gd` from the core's `strikes` and `events` and from what changed since the tick before, kept by person in packed arrays as the sound scene keeps them, in palette colours only: what fades thins by an ordered dither and blends nothing. The person marked as the target is the one the player selected while it lives, and with none selected the one the steered person's last use struck.
    - Each use that deals body damage flashes its shape for 160 ms where it lies on screen: a frontal arc of its range, `ARC_HALF_WIDTH_DEGREES` either side, a line to the far end the record gives with a projectile running along it, a targeted use as a line to its point, and an area or self use as a disc of its radius, each foreshortened down the screen as the ground is. It is drawn in its user's faction's accent, the steered person's in jade and wherever they are. Uses that strike only structures, a mend or a torch, show in the structures' stages alone.
    - A living person whose health fell is drawn in the fire ramp's impact core, thinning out over 120 ms, and knocked 2 pixels away from the nearest use that hit it as it does. The loss floats 12 pixels up over 800 ms, in paper with an ink shadow, and in crimson for the steered person's own, for the people the settings' damage numbers name: by default the steered person's own losses and those its uses hit, or everyone's, or no one's.
    - A person the core records dying on a tick a use hit it falls a quarter turn about its feet and fades where it last stood, over 700 ms, drawn in its row's band beside the living, its killing blow flashing and floating up as any loss does. One who died hit by no use, of hunger or age, is not drawn falling.
    - The person the steered person's last use hit nearest the point it went toward is its target, marked by crimson corner brackets while it lives, until 2 seconds pass without another.
- `sound/sound.tscn` plays what the core reports, as `docs/audio.md` sets out its sounds, their sources and their mixing. It holds no game rule: after each tick it reads the core's `events` recorded since the last, and for what the core records no event of, compares what the adapter reads with what it read on the tick before.
  - A death the core records is a fall where it happened, and a raid it records rings the bell at the raided village's seat.
  - A body whose `body_ready` moved struck, and a living body whose health fell was hit. A structure whose stage worsened caught fire, and one whose stage improved was mended. The core records no event of these.
  - Each is heard where it happened, when it lies within the part of the world on screen, through a pool of 12 voices on the Effects bus: at most one of each kind starts a tick, and what finds no free voice is dropped. A voice is free once its sound's length has passed on the scene's clock.
  - The music on the Music bus is the battle track while a strike was heard in the last 8 seconds, and the explore track otherwise. The screens' clicks and openings sound through a voice of their own.
  - The buses, Master with Effects and Music sending to it, are laid out in `default_bus_layout.tres`.
  - Leaving the tree, it stops every player and waits until the audio server has finished a mix, which releases what they played: in Godot 4.7.2 the server frees a stopped sound only in its next mix, and reports one still held at shutdown as leaked.
- `world_view/world_view.tscn` shows the M1 scenario through `world_art.tscn`, and sounds it through `sound/sound.tscn`, starting its camera at `m1_centre`, and ticks at the core's tick rate whatever the frame rate, drawing the bodies between ticks. It names the people round the lead of the army nearest `m1_centre` through `hud/nameplates.gd`, on a layer at the HUD's scale, so `make bench` measures a frame with names and rings on. `make bench` renders it.
- The view fills the window at a whole-pixel scale, drawing textures without filtering, as `view_scale.gd` sets it: on a PC, the largest multiple at which the world shows at least 640 x 360 of its pixels (2x at 1280 x 720, 3x at 1920 x 1080, 4x at 2560 x 1440), and with the touch controls, the largest at which it shows at least 480 x 270. The camera zooms by that multiple and the window shows as many world pixels as it holds at it. Bodies stand on whole world pixels, so the world is not drawn finer than its own pixels. `world_view.tscn` draws at the PC's scale, so `make bench` measures the PC's view.
- The world is presented at Albion's angle, a core cell drawn 16 pixels wide and 12 deep and raised by its level, with combat in real time on the core's tick. Ground and roofs are seen from above at that angle; facades and standing bodies are drawn upright and sorted by the row their footprint ends on. A structure is drawn over the footprint of width x height cells the core gives it, which `structure_footprint` reads back; `create` takes each army's members as cells and health, and no footprint, so every structure it makes is 1 x 1. Art is pixel art, from the Aseprite sources under `art/`, made to `docs/art-direction.md`. UI text is Korean first, with English supported.
- `date` reads the core's calendar back as `year`, `season` and `day`, the season counted from 0.
- `play/play.tscn`, the project's main scene, opens on the title screen, where a new game has `create_region` make the region of the seed typed, or of one drawn at random. Continue on the title resumes the world saved in slot 0. It steers the army `player_army` names when the world is made or loaded and after every tick, so the person its soul is born as after a rebirth; while no body carries the soul it keeps the army it last steered. A right click orders it toward the cell clicked, and while the button is held, toward the cell under the cursor, as the cursor or the camera moves. Keys 1 to 9 order its automatic strike to use that skill of its army's, and 0 all of them; an order keeps whatever part the input does not change. Q, W, E, R and T cast the skills on the loadout's five slots at the cursor's point through `cast`, the core bringing a point beyond the range within it: on the key's press, or, as the settings choose, on its release, while the key is held `play/aim.gd` drawing the skill's range round the person and its area at the cursor, at the ring for a cursor beyond it, as `art/mockup/mobile_hud_aim.png` draws them, foreshortened down the screen as the ground is; the same key pressed again or Escape cancels the aim. A cast the core refuses shows its reason from `cast_refusal` on the HUD, and an empty slot `CAST_NO_SKILL`'s. G opens the genealogy panel and M the sect panel, where Enter has the person apply to the sect shown, K the arts panel, where the arrows choose one of the person's skills, Q to T put it on that slot of the loadout, leaving any other slot it was on, and Delete takes it off, and F the faction window, where up and down choose a faction, left and right an order, Enter gives the order lit through `declare`, war asked again first, Enter declaring it and Escape taking it back, and C has the person challenge its faction's leader, marching on the leader, when `challenge_refusal` names no reason: a duel begun closes the window and the HUD announces it, and a refusal shows its word in the window. A left click on a foe selects it as the target, marking it in the world, on its name and in the HUD's target frame, and does not move the person; a left click on anyone else `talk_refusal` lets the player talk to opens the talk, and on the ground clears the selection, as Escape does; Tab selects the next foe in `foes_in_sight`, nearest first. Space sets an attack intent, as the person's order, on the selection, or with none on the nearest foe in sight, which it selects; a target `attack_refusal` names a reason for shows that reason on the HUD, and with no foe in sight Space does nothing. The core chases the target and strikes it until the intent ends; a move order replaces it, another selection moves it, and Escape, a click on the ground or a selection it may not attack ends it through `end_intent`; once the intent ends, whether the core or the player ends it, the person holds where it stands. A change of skills keeps the intent. Outside the faction window, C talks to the person the prompt over it names, the nearest living person `talk_refusal` lets the player talk to or refuses only for hostility, read once a tick, in the dialogue window, where up and down choose what to ask or scroll the news, Enter asks it, its wares showing what it sells, where up and down choose a ware and Enter buys it through `buy`, and Escape goes back from the news or the wares or ends the talk; the window closes on the tick `talk_refusal` stops being `TALK_NONE`, and the world runs on while it is open. Over a hostile person the prompt shows the refusal and C opens nothing. Z and X consume one of the first and second wares through `consume`, and the HUD notices the ware and what it restored, or the refusal. Escape, with none of them open, no aim held and no target selected, pauses the world under the pause panel. The pause panel's Save, and an autosave on the tick the third in-game day since the last save begins, write slot 0, once a body carries the player's soul, and the loadout beside it in `loadout_0.cfg` of the saves' directory, which Continue reads with the slot; the days between autosaves are a constant of `play.gd`. Each key is bound once, as a constant of `play.gd`, and the pause panel's guide is drawn from those constants. The world ticks at the core's tick rate times the speed the settings choose, and no tick runs while a screen is open. Its camera follows the person where its body is drawn, rounded to the pixel as the body is, and keeps the last place it stood while no body serves in its army; the aim and the touch controls are drawn from there too. When the tick on which the person died finds its soul born again as another, the play scene opens the rebirth screen on both, and no further tick runs that frame. It draws the world through `world_art.tscn` and the HUD through `hud/hud.tscn`, and sounds it through `sound/sound.tscn`, where a screen or panel opening and a key or a tap a screen takes sound too.
  - On a PC, the HUD's shortcut rail lists `SHORTCUTS`, the play scene's one table of what each row opens, its key, its icon and its word: genealogy (G), sect (M), factions (F), arts (K), the settings, which no key opens, and pause (Esc). The pause panel's guide lists the rows with a key from the same table, after the other controls. A left click on a row presses its key through the same path a key takes, so it does exactly what the key does wherever it is pressed: with a panel open, its own row closes it and another row's key does what that panel does with it. The settings' row opens the settings. A click off the rail does nothing.
  - While the HUD's touch controls are shown, a finger steers the person instead, and a click gives no order. The joystick orders it the nearest of the eight ways it points, three cells that way or as many fewer as the core accepts, again from each new cell it reaches while held, and let go, to stop; a diagonal is walked on its line, as the right button's cell down one is. A skill button casts the skill on its slot of the loadout, the same five Q to T cast; one whose slot is empty casts nothing: tapped, at the person `aim` finds facing the way the joystick last pointed, or with none, at the point that way at the skill's range; dragged, at the point the drag aims, its share of the button's pad times the skill's range from the person, which the core brings within the range; dropped on the cancel zone, at nothing; held for a long press, at the person itself. It casts through the same path as Q, W, E, R and T: `cast_refusal` is read first, and the HUD's notice names its reason when it refuses. ## Rationale

- Godot is the presentation adapter and the core reaches it through GDExtension: ADR 0002 (`adr/0002-build-on-godot-with-a-cpp-simulation-core.md`).
- The binding release, its pin, the build path, the scenario format, how refusals reach GDScript, the test runner and how CI installs Godot: ADR 0004 (`adr/0004-bind-the-core-to-godot-with-godot-cpp-through-cmake.md`).
- The calendar kept by the core and read by the HUD, and the player's body carrying a soul: ADR 0019 (`adr/0019-draw-talent-and-lifespan-at-birth-and-bear-a-soul-again.md`).
- Every body one person, read and drawn one by one, and no focus to set: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`).
- Saving through the core's save port, served by files under `user://saves/`, and what the adapter regenerates from the region's origin on load: ADR 0030 (`adr/0030-save-a-world-through-a-save-port-in-the-cores-own-versioned-encoding.md`).
- Cross-building the Windows library with a pinned llvm-mingw, and the alternatives weighed: ADR 0033 (`adr/0033-cross-build-the-windows-extension-with-a-pinned-llvm-mingw.md`).
- Applying to a sect through the core's thresholds, and the rumours read from the core's ring of events: ADR 0031 (`adr/0031-let-a-person-apply-to-a-sect-by-the-admissions-thresholds.md`) and ADR 0032 (`adr/0032-keep-a-bounded-ring-of-world-events-and-read-rumours-from-it.md`).
- Steering through the orders every army follows: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).
- Casting through the core at a point, a person or itself, and a tap's target read from the core with the facing the adapter gives: ADR 0034 (`adr/0034-let-a-person-cast-a-skill-at-a-point-a-person-or-itself.md`).
- The faction window, the nameplates and the ring round a hostile body reading the core's one relation, declarations given only by a faction's leader through the core's port, and the player's army marked steered so the core's diplomacy decides nothing for it: ADR 0038 (`adr/0038-let-faction-leaders-declare-war-and-make-peace-over-their-stances.md`).

## Open questions

- **Banners after a change of owner.** A banner keeps the colours of the owner its village had when it was laid, while the map's square and the borders follow the owner now.
- **The map on the rail.** The rail has no map row until #168 gives the map a window and a key; that change adds the row to `SHORTCUTS`, and the rail and the pause guide both list it.
- **Panels by touch.** On a touch screen the genealogy and sect panels open and close, but their selection, Tab's next faction and Enter's application have no touch of their own yet.
- **Talking by touch.** On a touch screen no button talks and no prompt shows, while touch is deferred (scope, 2026-10-09); a tap only closes an open dialogue window.
- **Wares by touch.** On a touch screen no belt shows and no button consumes a ware, while touch is deferred (scope, 2026-10-09); the silver strip shows.
- **Touch layout on a wide screen.** With the touch controls, the HUD keeps its 480 x 270 layout centred, so on a screen wider than 16:9 the world fills the sides while the joystick and the skill buttons stand inward of the screen's edges.
- **Bodies hidden by bodies.** A body hidden by another body is not outlined, and one outlined behind a building is outlined over another body standing between them too. The ground has no quality profile of ADR 0048's yet (#212).
- **Levels in the core.** The relief is derived by the view until ADR 0047's map carries levels (#173), which #214's climbing and sight need.
- **Inner energy.** The core has none. The HUD's inner-energy bar stands empty with a dash for its value until the core holds it.

- **Content from packs.** ADR 0049 (`adr/0049-load-the-games-content-from-data-packs-and-let-mods-be-packs-over-the-base.md`), proposed; nothing above changes until the pull requests that build it land.
  - **Packs.** The extension passes the bytes Godot's file access reads from the base pack and from `user://mods/` to the pack reader, which parses them into the core's terms.
  - **Text.** `hud/hud.csv` becomes each pack's `text/` documents per locale, given to the translation server when the packs load, since Godot imports a CSV only in the editor and at export.
  - **Art.** Faction accents and sprite sets, and each nature axis value's colour, come from the packs' `view/` documents, apart from the hashed data, in place of `hud/palette.gd`'s accents, `hud/affiliation.gd`'s list of sprite sets and `hud/arts.gd`'s ramp by orientation.
  - **Mods.** A mods screen lists the mods found, with their order, errors and overlaps, and the new-game screen shows the packs refused.
- **Gear and the economy's screens.** ADRs 0050 to 0052, proposed. The body is drawn in layers, body, armour, head and weapon, with armour and headgear tinted by the wearer's faction. New screens:
  - an inventory and equipment window on I
  - a warehouse window at a keeper or a cave
  - a cave's entrance drawn on the ground and the maps only once the player's soul has found it, by a search order or by stepping on it
  - a prompt and a list for piles on the ground
  - a market window, replacing the wares screen
  - a household panel
  - a treasury window for a leader
- **Standing in the view.** ADR 0054, proposed. The dialogue offers attack, extort, threaten, help and give, with their refusals. Its card and the faction window show the player's standing with the village, sect and faction by tier. A body whose groups stand hostile toward the player is ringed crimson like a hostile faction's, and an unfriendly one in a paler ring.
- **The lineage window.** ADR 0055, proposed. A window on L, apart from the K loadout panel, lays out the arts' graph by family and tier. It shows known arts with their stage and mastery, arts known of dimmed, the rest as ???, and edges with the stages they need. Its mockup goes to the PM before it is built.
- **Cliffs in the view and the input.** ADR 0058, proposed. A cliff is lifted 16 pixels a level for its first three levels and 6 a level beyond, in place of ADR 0059's 16 at every height, so a ten-level cliff is 90 pixels high; those numbers are the pack's `view/` data. Ground the viewer cannot see, read from the core's veil per area, is drawn as terrain outline only, with no people, items or structures. While the core recomputes it, up to about 40 ticks after the viewer moves, the view keeps drawing the last complete veil. A cliff the viewer can leap up is never veiled. A leap is drawn as a jump, up and down alike, with a stamina bar under a body below full stamina. Clicking a cell on top of a cliff the body can leap up, or below one it can jump down, walks there and leaps; a cell beyond reach is refused with its reason shown. Until the map's levels reach the core, the view derives levels for itself, as the 2.5D mockup does (#200, #204).
- **Natural objects in the view.** ADR 0056, proposed. The view draws each slot of the areas in its chunks, from the core's slot table: its kind's sprite for its stage, from the pack's `view/`, redrawn when a slot's stage changes. A grown tree's canopy is drawn upright, like a structure's facade.
- **Zooming out for world-scale arts.** ADR 0057, proposed. The camera zooms out to a half, a quarter, an eighth and a sixteenth. The ground is drawn from downscaled area images built with the map and redrawn for changed areas, with people as dots below a quarter and as one cluster per area below an eighth. A wave's aim shows its full disc, and the cast pulls the camera back. An area whose terrain changes has its ground re-baked once after the change settles, spread over frames, which extends ADR 0059's bake once per area.
- **Animals in the view.** ADR 0067, proposed. The view draws each animal of the areas in its chunks from the core's animal store, by kind and state, with sprites from the pack's `view/`: grazing, fleeing, fighting and dying. A spirit beast's territory is shown once the player has seen its beast. Hunting needs no new input: the player strikes an animal, or takes it as a cast's target.
- **Riders and boats in the view.** ADR 0061, proposed. A rider is drawn on its horse, and a boat as a vehicle on the river's cells with its passengers aboard. `mount`, `dismount` and boarding are orders through the port, with their refusals shown.
- **Which platforms the library is built for.** It is built for the host, macOS arm64 and Linux x86_64 so far, for Android arm64, and for the PC exports, macOS universal and Windows x86_64; iOS, Linux and Windows arm64 exports are not built.
