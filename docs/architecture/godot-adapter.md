# Godot adapter

The GDExtension adapter through which Godot drives the simulation core: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- The adapter is a GDExtension library built from `extension/`, linking `core/` unchanged. `murim.gdextension` at the repository root loads it into the Godot project there.
- `make build` builds the library for the host. `make android` builds it for Android arm64 through the NDK's CMake toolchain file, with the same `extension/CMakeLists.txt`, and exports two debug APKs from the same library: the `Android` preset's, which opens the game on the title screen, and the `Android bench` preset's, whose launch runs `world_view/frame_bench.gd`. `make export` builds release libraries for macOS arm64 and x86_64 in one universal library, and for Windows x86_64 by cross-building with a pinned llvm-mingw, and exports a release build for each that opens on the title screen.
- It registers one class, `WorldAdapter`, which creates a core world, ticks it, and reads back its hash, its date, each army's members' health when created, together, its faction and skills now, the positions and health of its living members, one person each, and each structure's cell, footprint, kind and stage. GDScript cannot construct it; `WorldAdapter.create`, `WorldAdapter.create_m1`, `WorldAdapter.create_region` and `WorldAdapter.load` are the only ways to get one.
- `create_m1` builds the core's M1 benchmark scenario (`core/m1_scenario.cpp`), the same one the tick benchmark runs; `m1_centre` gives the point its battle is fought around.
- `create_region` builds the region the core generates from a seed, with its villages, each faction's leadership and its rules of life, and one person born into a village, the first unless another is named, as the last army. Its days last the core's 1,800 ticks unless another length is given, which `Rules::ticks_per_day` takes; a day of no ticks is refused with null. That person carries a soul, so when it dies it is born again into the same army. It keeps what the view draws of the region: each cell's terrain kind, the kinds' and factions' names, and the villages, read back by `terrain`, `terrain_names`, `faction_names` and `villages`, each village with the name of its template and its owner now. `villages` lists the region's villages, then those founded as the world runs, each with the kind, corner and area the core's `settlement` gives and named as the region numbers its own. `territory` reads each cell's faction as the core derives it now, or -1 for a cell no village claims. Its structures carry their kinds, and so do the M1 scenario's, which are barrels. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no region, and these return empty.
- `save` writes the world to a slot through the core's save port, served by `FileSaves`, which keeps slot `n` as `slot_<n>.sav` in a directory: `user://saves`, which `saves_directory` gives, unless `save`, `load` or `load_refusal` is given another, as the tests give `user://test_saves`; it refuses a world made by `create` or `create_m1`, which has no region, with `push_error` and false. `WorldAdapter.load` reads a slot back: it regenerates what the view draws from the region's seed and the player's birth village, which the save holds, and returns null after `push_error` naming the core's `LoadError`. `load_refusal` gives that `LoadError` as one of the `LOAD_*` constants, `LOAD_NOT_A_SAVE` for a save of a world with no region, or `LOAD_NONE` when the core reads the slot; it decodes the save without regenerating the region. `player_army` gives the army the living person carrying the player's soul serves in, read from the core, so a loaded world finds its player with no field of its own, or -1 while none lives. `region_seed` gives the seed the region was generated from, or -1 for a world with no region.
- `order` passes an order to the core: an army, an objective cell as a `Vector2i`, skill indices as a `PackedInt64Array`, and optionally a faction whose leader its lead challenges. An order the core refuses makes it report the refusal with `push_error` and return false. `map_rows` and `skill_count` read back what a driver needs to aim one.
- Leadership is read back as the core holds it:
  - `leader` gives each faction's leading person, `elects` whether the faction elects its leader, and `duel` the two people fighting for a faction's lead.
  - `challenge_refusal` gives the core's verdict on a challenge before it is made, one of the `CHALLENGE_*` constants.
  - `hostile`, `roster`, `person_position` and `person_health` read what a duel changes and who fights it.
- Each person's name and kin are read back as the core holds them: `person_name` gives its surname and given name in Korean and romanised, `names` every surname and female and male given name the world's rules of life were given, in Korean and romanised, empty for a world from `create`, and `person_parents`, `person_children`, `person_spouse` (the last it married) and `person_family` its kin; `person_age` its age in whole years of the core's calendar, and `person_faction` the faction of the army it serves in, or -1 for one serving in none, as the dead do. The genealogy panel and the HUD read them.
- Each village's cap, stock and population are read back as the core derives them by `village_cap`, `village_stock` and `village_population`, villages founded as the world runs included, and each field's state by `structure_field_state`, one of the `FIELD_*` constants or -1 for a structure that is no field.
- Sects and manuals are read back as the core holds them: `sect_count`, `person_sect` (-1 for none), `sect_name`, `sect_faction`, `sect_seat`, `sect_seat_held`, `sect_members` and `sect_elders` as person indices, `sect_master` (-1 for none), `sect_cap` and `sect_stands`, and `manuals`, each a Dictionary of its `art` and its `place`, "sect", "cell" or "lost", with its `sect` or `cell`. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no sects or manuals.
- A person applies to a sect through the core: `application_refusal` gives the core's verdict, one of the `APPLICATION_*` constants, and `apply` makes the person a member, or reports the core's refusal with `push_error` and returns false.
- Qi is read back as the core holds it: `person_qi` and `person_qi_capacity`, `person_realm`, the rank, `realms`, each realm's name in Korean and romanised, its qi and its might, `person_nature`, the orientation and polarity of a person's heart method as `ORIENTATION_*` and `POLARITY_*` constants, empty while it knows no art, and `person_skills`, the skills a person may cast by the index `cast` takes, its army's and then the art skills it knows, each with its cost, its ready tick and, for an art skill, its art, its index among the art's skills, rank, name and nature. `arts` gives every art of the world's data, its name in Korean and romanised, its nature and its skills in the order they are learned, each with its name, the rank it needs, its range, radius, cooldown and cost, so the arts panel lists the skills a person has not learned too. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no realms or arts, and its people hold no qi.
- A person casts through the core: `cast` takes a person, a skill by index among `person_skills`, and a target, a `Vector2` point in sub-cell units, an int person or null for itself, and reports the core's refusal, or a target of another type, with `push_error` and false. `cast_refusal` gives the core's verdict first, one of the `CAST_*` constants, or -1 after `push_error` for a target of another type. `aim` gives the hostile person a skill would aim at, favouring a facing given as a `Vector2`, or -1 for none. `skills` gives each skill's range and cooldown, `body_ready` when each body may use one again, and `tick_count` the tick the world has reached, which a ready tick is counted against, for the controls' indicators.
- `rumours` reads the events that reach a person as the core derives them, newest first, each a Dictionary of its `sequence`, `tick`, `cell` and `kind`, "founded", "raided", "ruined", "sect_fell", "leader_changed", "born" or "died", with the records it names. `person_attainment` reads a person's attainment by discipline, and `discipline_names` the disciplines the world's rules of life draw, empty for a world from `create` or `create_m1`.
- `body_ready` gives the tick from which each of an army's living members may strike again, which moves on each strike, in the order of `body_positions`. `events` gives the events the core keeps from a given sequence on, oldest first, each with its sequence, tick, cell and kind, one of the `EVENT_*` constants: founded, raided, ruined, sect fell, leader changed, born or died. The sound scene reads them. `progress` gives the steps of progress the core keeps from a given sequence on, oldest first, each with its sequence, tick, kind, one of the `PROGRESS_*` constants, learned or realm gained, and person; a skill learned also gives its art, the skill's index in the art and how it was learned, one of the `LEARNED_BY_*` constants, comprehension, manual or teaching, and a realm gained its rank. The HUD reads them for its notices.
- `army_count` and `structure_count` count what the world holds now, the armies and structures its villages' growth adds included. A structure growth laid takes its plan's kind in `structure_kind`. `army_strength` gives 0 for an army formed as the world runs.
- Positions cross in sub-cell units as `Vector2`s, exact while a coordinate stays within 2^24. The tick rate, the cell size and the structure stages cross as the class's integer constants, so GDScript repeats none of them.
- `create` takes what the core's constructor takes, as Godot types: a seed, map rows as a `PackedStringArray`, and the armies (with their skills), structures, rules and, optionally, each faction's leadership as `Dictionary`s keyed by the core's field names, with cells as `Vector2i`. Converting them to the core's types, and the hash back to a signed int, is the adapter's work.
- Input the core refuses, or that is missing, of the wrong type or outside the core's integer types, makes `create` report it with `push_error` and return null. No exception crosses into Godot.
- It holds no game rule: every call is passed to the core, which decides what is valid.
- `world_art/world_art.tscn` draws a world in the approved art, and both scenes below use it. It holds no game rule.
  - Terrain is laid as `docs/convention/stack-godot.md` §Terrain layers sets out. A world without a region draws its passable cells as plain and its blocked cells as mountain.
  - Territory borders are a dotted line in each owner's accent, on the side of each cell a faction holds, drawn from `territory`. Each tick reads it and redraws them, and signals the change, only when it moved or a village was founded. Landforms are `Sprite2D`s.
  - Each structure is a `Sprite2D` over its footprint, showing its stage's frame of its kind's art: house, hall, well, barrels, fence, granary, storehouse, training ground, manual hall, seat gate, smithy or apothecary. Each kind's frame holds its footprint as `docs/art-direction.md` sets out, the seat gate's higher than the rest. A training ground is laid with the ground, under everything standing, so the people on it are drawn over it. A structure from `create` has no kind and is not drawn. A structure growth lays is given its sprite on the next refresh, and an army growth forms is read on the next tick.
  - Each village's decorations come from `world_art/villages.json` by its template's name: paving and road as ground tiles on a layer above the terrain, a seat's paved court and its path to the gate among them, and banners in its owner's colours standing with everything else: a village's banner, and a seat's sect banners. They never block or break. A founded village's are laid on the tick that finds it, as a generated one's are.
  - Each field is tiled from `art/decorations/fields` by the state the core gives it, sown, growing, ripe, fallow or burnt, with its winter tag in winter, re-tiled when its state or the season changes.
  - Bodies are drawn only on screen, through one `MultiMesh` per row of cells, from one texture holding every faction's sprites. Each row's bounds span the region's width, so a row refilled after the view moves is not culled by where it was first drawn. Every body is one person, drawn as its faction's sprite, walking and facing the way it moved since the last tick.
  - The rows, the structures and the landforms are sorted together by the bottom edge of what they stand on. The steered person carries the player's ring and chevron.
- `sound/sound.tscn` plays what the core reports, as `docs/audio.md` sets out its sounds, their sources and their mixing. It holds no game rule: after each tick it reads the core's `events` recorded since the last, and for what the core records no event of, compares what the adapter reads with what it read on the tick before.
  - A death the core records is a fall where it happened, and a raid it records rings the bell at the raided village's seat.
  - A body whose `body_ready` moved struck, and a living body whose health fell was hit. A structure whose stage worsened caught fire, and one whose stage improved was mended. The core records no event of these.
  - Each is heard where it happened, when it lies within the part of the world on screen, through a pool of 12 voices on the Effects bus: at most one of each kind starts a tick, and what finds no free voice is dropped. A voice is free once its sound's length has passed on the scene's clock.
  - The music on the Music bus is the battle track while a strike was heard in the last 8 seconds, and the explore track otherwise. The screens' clicks and openings sound through a voice of their own.
  - The buses, Master with Effects and Music sending to it, are laid out in `default_bus_layout.tres`.
  - Leaving the tree, it stops every player and waits until the audio server has finished a mix, which releases what they played: in Godot 4.7.2 the server frees a stopped sound only in its next mix, and reports one still held at shutdown as leaked.
- `world_view/world_view.tscn` shows the M1 scenario through `world_art.tscn`, and sounds it through `sound/sound.tscn`, starting its camera at `m1_centre`, and ticks at the core's tick rate whatever the frame rate. `make bench` renders it.
- The view renders at 480 x 270 and scales to the window by whole multiples, drawing textures without filtering.
- The world is presented in a three-quarter top-down view, one core cell to one 16-pixel tile, with combat in real time on the core's tick. Ground and roofs are seen from above; facades and standing bodies are drawn upright and sorted by the bottom edge of their footprint. A structure is drawn over the footprint of width x height cells the core gives it, which `structure_footprint` reads back; `create` takes each army's members as cells and health, and no footprint, so every structure it makes is 1 x 1. Art is pixel art on that grid, from the Aseprite sources under `art/`, made to `docs/art-direction.md`. UI text is Korean first, with English supported.
- `date` reads the core's calendar back as `year`, `season` and `day`, the season counted from 0.
- `play/play.tscn`, the project's main scene, opens on the title screen, where a new game has `create_region` make the region of the seed typed, or of one drawn at random. Continue on the title resumes the world saved in slot 0. It steers the army `player_army` names when the world is made or loaded and after every tick, so the person its soul is born as after a rebirth; while no body carries the soul it keeps the army it last steered. A right click orders it toward the cell clicked, and while the button is held, toward the cell under the cursor, as the cursor or the camera moves. Keys 1 to 9 order its automatic strike to use that skill of its army's, and 0 all of them; an order keeps whatever part the input does not change. Q, W, E, R and T cast the skills on the loadout's five slots at the cursor's point through `cast`, the core bringing a point beyond the range within it: on the key's press, or, as the settings choose, on its release, while the key is held `play/aim.gd` drawing the skill's range round the person and its area at the cursor, at the ring for a cursor beyond it, as `art/mockup/mobile_hud_aim.png` draws them; the same key pressed again or Escape cancels the aim. A cast the core refuses shows its reason from `cast_refusal` on the HUD, and an empty slot `CAST_NO_SKILL`'s. C has it challenge its faction's leader, marching on the leader, when `challenge_refusal` names no reason; either way the HUD shows the verdict. G opens the genealogy panel and M the sect panel, where Enter has the person apply to the sect shown, and K the arts panel, where the arrows choose one of the person's skills, Q to T put it on that slot of the loadout, leaving any other slot it was on, and Delete takes it off. Escape, with none of them open and no aim held, pauses the world under the pause panel. The pause panel's Save, and an autosave on the tick the third in-game day since the last save begins, write slot 0, once a body carries the player's soul, and the loadout beside it in `loadout_0.cfg` of the saves' directory, which Continue reads with the slot; the days between autosaves are a constant of `play.gd`. Each key is bound once, as a constant of `play.gd`, and the pause panel's guide is drawn from those constants. The world ticks at the core's tick rate times the speed the settings choose, and no tick runs while a screen is open. Its camera follows the person, and keeps the last place it stood while no body serves in its army. When the tick on which the person died finds its soul born again as another, the play scene opens the rebirth screen on both, and no further tick runs that frame. It draws the world through `world_art.tscn` and the HUD through `hud/hud.tscn`, and sounds it through `sound/sound.tscn`, where a screen or panel opening and a key or a tap a screen takes sound too.
  - While the HUD's touch controls are shown, a finger steers the person instead, and a click gives no order. The joystick orders it the nearest of the eight ways it points, three cells that way or as many fewer as the core accepts, again from each new cell it reaches while held, and let go, to stop. A skill button casts the skill on its slot of the loadout, the same five Q to T cast; one whose slot is empty casts nothing: tapped, at the person `aim` finds facing the way the joystick last pointed, or with none, at the point that way at the skill's range; dragged, at the point the drag aims, its share of the button's pad times the skill's range from the person, which the core brings within the range; dropped on the cancel zone, at nothing; held for a long press, at the person itself. It casts through the same path as Q, W, E, R and T: `cast_refusal` is read first, and the HUD's notice names its reason when it refuses. The genealogy, sect, duel and arts buttons open those panels and challenge as G, M, C and K do, and a tap closes an open genealogy or sect panel; the pause button pauses. While the arts panel is open, a tap on a tile chooses its skill, a skill button tapped puts the skill chosen on that button's slot, a finger moved up or down 20 pixels pages the tiles, and the pause button's place closes it. With tap-to-move chosen in the settings, a tap on the ground orders the person to that cell. The play scene gives the touch controls where the person stands on screen.
- `hud/genealogy.gd`, the genealogy panel, opens on the steered person with G, as `art/mockup/genealogy_panel.png` lays it out. It shows a three-generation tree: up to two parents, the person and their spouse, and up to three children. Its header names the family and the centred person's generation in it: 1 for someone with no parents, as the people a world begins with have none, and otherwise one more than their parents' deepest, counted from `person_parents`. Each card has a portrait in the person's faction's body (greyed once dead), a name, and an age or that they died. A column holds the person's name, family, sect, age, spouse and children, with talent and lifespan shown unknown, since they are hidden. Arrows move the selection, Enter centres the tree on it, and G or Escape closes the panel; while it is open, no key gives an order. Its words come from `hud/hud.csv`; English cards carry the given name alone, the full name standing in the column. A name or word wider than the room where it is drawn is cut short with an ellipsis.
- `hud/sect.gd`, the sect panel, opens with M on the steered person's sect, or on the first sect of its faction while it belongs to none, as `art/mockup/sect_panel.png` lays it out. It shows the sect's name and faction, and whether it is the player's; its seat's gate in the stage the gate's structure is in, and whether the seat is held, lost or the sect fallen, from `sect_seat_held` and `sect_stands`; its master, its elders' count and its members against its cap, one pip a place; and the cards of its master and up to three other elders. A column shows a faction's leader and that leader's sect, a tag when the faction elects its leader, read from `elects`, or else when its lead passes by duel, read as `challenge_refusal` naming any reason but `CHALLENGE_NO_SUCCESSION`, and the faction's sects with their members against their caps, or that the seat is lost or the sect fallen. Up and down choose among the faction's sects, Tab shows the next faction, Enter has the steered person apply to the sect shown, its verdict from `application_refusal` drawn under the cards and cleared when another sect or faction is shown, and M or Escape closes the panel; while it is open, no key gives an order. Its words come from `hud/hud.csv`, the sects' names keyed by the core's, and the English faction tabs are shortened to fit a third of the column. It and the genealogy panel share their frame, cards and fitting in `hud/panel.gd`.
- `hud/arts.gd`, the arts panel, opens with K, or the arts button, on the steered person, as `art/mockup/arts_panel.png` lays it out on PC and `arts_panel_touch.png` on a touch screen, in the panels' frame. Its left column on PC is 118 pixels, 16 wider than the mockup's, so the longest English realm and art names fit beside their labels. It shows the person's realm by name on a pip for each of `realms`, the next realm and the qi that reaches it, or the qi alone where both overrun the column, its qi against its store, and its heart method, the art it learned first, with that art's nature; then each art it knows a skill of, in the order first learned, with its nature and a tag, the heart method's or, for an art of another nature, weaker, and every skill of it from `arts`: its qi cost, the realm it needs, in crimson while above the person's, and its cooldown in seconds, greyed while not learned; then its army's skills, named by what they do, striking, mending or burning, at no cost and with the shared cooldown. Each skill on a slot shows its key. On PC, ten of the list's rows show at once and the list scrolls to keep the chosen skill in view, with the loadout's five slots along the foot; on a touch screen, three arts' tiles show at once, with the tapped skill's art, nature, cost, realm, cooldown, range and area beside them, while the touch controls draw the arc. The arrows choose only among the skills the person has, and a tile of one it lacks chooses nothing. Every icon is the technique bar's, `burst` or `strike`: the mockups' icons of their own for each art skill are not in the production set. Names come from the core in Korean, or romanised in English.
- `hud/loadout.gd`, the loadout, holds the skill on each of the five slots by what it is, an army's skill by its index among its army's skills and an art skill by its art and its index in the art, so a slot keeps its skill as the person learns more. It follows one person: another, as after a rebirth or on a new game, starts from its first five skills. It is the player's choice, not the world's state, so the core's save does not hold it; the play scene writes it with every save to `loadout_0.cfg` beside slot 0 and reads it on Continue, so it comes back with the world it was saved with, and a loadout changed after the last save goes back with the world.
- `hud/title.gd`, `hud/new_game.gd`, `hud/pause.gd` and `hud/settings.gd`, the screens, are drawn as `art/mockup/title_screen.png`, `new_game_screen.png`, `pause_screen.png` and `settings_screen.png` lay them out, in the genealogy and sect panels' frame from `hud/panel.gd`, which also draws their menus, choices' slips and keys' caps. The screen open on top takes every key and every tap, and the play scene acts on what it returns: a tap on an entry takes it, a tap on a choice chooses it, and a tap on the close word or outside the panel goes back.
  - The title screen draws `art/title/backdrop.png`, the mountains and the working title, with a line and a menu over it: a new game, continue, which cannot be chosen while `load_refusal` finds slot 0 missing, the settings and quit. When a save is not loaded, a line under the menu names the `LoadError` why.
  - The new-game panel takes a seed of up to ten digits, or draws one at random with R, a tap on its random slip, or when its field is left empty; a tap on its field brings up the screen's keyboard for digits.
  - The pause panel's menu resumes, saves, opens the settings, leaves for the title by reloading the scene, or quits. A line under it says whether the save was written. The HUD's notice says whether an autosave was. It shows the world's seed, and the controls guide: each control's keys on their caps and what it does, as the play scene binds them.
  - The settings panel chooses the time speed, 1x, 2x or 4x; the window mode, windowed or fullscreen; the window's size, 2x, 3x or 4x the native 480 x 270; the language; the volume of the Master, Effects and Music buses, each 0%, 25%, 50%, 75% or 100%, 0% muting the bus; whether a cast key casts on its press, the default, or on its release; and the touch controls, shown automatically on a phone or a tablet (Godot's `mobile` feature), on, on with tap-to-move, or off. A desktop's touch screen is not asked after, since Godot 4.7.2 reports one wherever a mouse stands in for a finger, as it does while the touch controls are shown at a desk. Each choice is applied and kept at once in `user://settings.cfg`, a preference of the player's and not the world's state. The play scene reads the file and applies it when it starts, and a choice the file lacks or holds out of range keeps its default.
  - `hud/rebirth.gd`, the rebirth screen, has no mockup and is drawn in the same frame. It shows the life just ended, the person's card greyed, its family, years lived, sect or none, art and children, beside the life begun, the new body's card, family, faction and age; Enter or Escape goes on.
  - Their words come from `hud/hud.csv`, with each screen's keys under a prefix of its own.
- `hud/touch.gd`, the touch controls, is drawn as `art/mockup/mobile_hud_idle.png`, `mobile_hud_aim.png`, `mobile_hud_cancel.png` and `mobile_hud_self.png` lay them out after `art/mockup/touch.lua`, with its places and sizes. It turns each touch into what the play scene does, and holds no game rule.
  - A joystick at the bottom left, which a thumb anywhere in its 200 x 130 zone puts under itself.
  - Five round skill buttons in an arc at the bottom right, the first larger, one for each of the loadout's slots, each with its skill's icon, `burst` or `strike` as the technique bar's, or a cross for an empty slot, which casts nothing. While the body cools down, every button is dimmed, the share of the wait left darkened clockwise from the top, with the seconds left over it, from `body_ready` and `tick_count`: the core's cooldown is the body's, not a skill's.
  - A touch moved more than 6 pixels is a drag, and one held still for 500 ms a long press. While a skill is dragged, its range is a dashed ring round the body, a dotted line runs to where it aims and the skill's area lies there, and the cancel zone shows on the arc's upper arm; on the zone, the rings turn stone and the button crimson. While held for a long press, a ring lies round the body and "on self" stands over the button.
  - Rings on the ground are drawn round, not flattened as the mockups draw them, since a cell is square on screen and the core measures a range the same each way.
  - The genealogy, sect, duel and arts buttons under the status panel, the arts button as `art/mockup/mobile_hud_arts.png` adds it, and the pause button in the top-right corner.
  - While the arts panel is open, as `art/mockup/arts_panel_touch.png` draws it, only the arc is drawn and touched, every button ringed in jade with a slip asking for one while a skill waits to be placed, and the pause button's place is a close button.
- `hud/hud.tscn` shows the player's portrait and faction; health as a percentage of its health when born over its bar, and its qi against its store, their thousands grouped, over the bar of the share it holds; its realm by name, or a dash for none, as `art/mockup/mobile_hud_arts.png` shows them; a map of the region around the player, drawn from `territory` and `villages` and redrawn when `world_art.tscn` signals a change, with the season and year from the core's calendar; a rumour log of the latest three of the player's `rumours`, newest first, each worded by its kind as a headline naming its factions, sect or person, or a line saying none has come, above the technique bar's row; its faction's leader, with C to challenge; while a duel for the lead is fought, both duellists and their health against what each had when it began, and when it ends whether the lead changed hands; the verdict on the player's challenge, or why it was refused; why the core refused the player's cast; each skill the player learned, worded by how, comprehension, a manual or its sect's teaching, with its name, and each realm it gained, read from `progress` and shown one after another; and the technique bar, the loadout's five slots, each with its key, Q, W, E, R or T, in a column of its own left of the icon of the skill on it, `burst` when its radius reaches past its target and `strike` otherwise, or none for an empty slot. The number keys order the automatic strike and are on no slot. ## Rationale

- Godot is the presentation adapter and the core reaches it through GDExtension: ADR 0002 (`adr/0002-build-on-godot-with-a-cpp-simulation-core.md`).
- The binding release, its pin, the build path, the scenario format, how refusals reach GDScript, the test runner and how CI installs Godot: ADR 0004 (`adr/0004-bind-the-core-to-godot-with-godot-cpp-through-cmake.md`).
- The calendar kept by the core and read by the HUD, and the player's body carrying a soul: ADR 0019 (`adr/0019-draw-talent-and-lifespan-at-birth-and-bear-a-soul-again.md`).
- Every body one person, read and drawn one by one, and no focus to set: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`).
- Saving through the core's save port, served by files under `user://saves/`, and what the adapter regenerates from the region's origin on load: ADR 0030 (`adr/0030-save-a-world-through-a-save-port-in-the-cores-own-versioned-encoding.md`).
- Cross-building the Windows library with a pinned llvm-mingw, and the alternatives weighed: ADR 0033 (`adr/0033-cross-build-the-windows-extension-with-a-pinned-llvm-mingw.md`).
- Applying to a sect through the core's thresholds, and the rumours the HUD reads from the core's ring of events: ADR 0031 (`adr/0031-let-a-person-apply-to-a-sect-by-the-admissions-thresholds.md`) and ADR 0032 (`adr/0032-keep-a-bounded-ring-of-world-events-and-read-rumours-from-it.md`).
- Steering through the orders every army follows: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).
- Casting through the core at a point, a person or itself, and a tap's target read from the core with the facing the adapter gives: ADR 0034 (`adr/0034-let-a-person-cast-a-skill-at-a-point-a-person-or-itself.md`).
- Qi, realms, natures and a person's skills read as the core holds them, and casts indexed among the skills a person knows: ADR 0035 (`adr/0035-cultivate-qi-into-realms-and-gain-arts-by-comprehension-manuals-and-teaching.md`).
- The HUD's notices of lessons and realms read from the core's store of progress, apart from the events rumours read: ADR 0036 (`adr/0036-keep-progress-in-the-martial-arts-in-a-store-of-its-own.md`).
- The three-quarter top-down view, structure footprints, real-time combat, pixel art on a 16-pixel tile grid and Korean-first UI text, and the alternatives rejected: ADR 0012 (`adr/0012-present-the-world-in-a-three-quarter-top-down-view.md`), which supersedes ADR 0008 (`adr/0008-present-the-world-top-down-in-real-time-as-pixel-art.md`).

## Open questions

- **Banners after a change of owner.** A banner keeps the colours of the owner its village had when it was laid, while the map's square and the borders follow the owner now.
- **Panels by touch.** On a touch screen the genealogy and sect panels open and close, but their selection, Tab's next faction and Enter's application have no touch of their own yet.
- **Rumours on a phone.** The touch layout's rumour log gives each line the mockup's 156 pixels, which cut short most rumours naming a person and some English ones about raids.
- **Inner energy.** The core has none. The HUD's inner-energy bar stands empty with a dash for its value until the core holds it.

- **Which platforms the library is built for.** It is built for the host, macOS arm64 and Linux x86_64 so far, for Android arm64, and for the PC exports, macOS universal and Windows x86_64; iOS, Linux and Windows arm64 exports are not built.
