# Godot adapter

The GDExtension adapter through which Godot drives the simulation core: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- The adapter is a GDExtension library built from `extension/`, linking `core/` unchanged. `murim.gdextension` at the repository root loads it into the Godot project there.
- It registers one class, `WorldAdapter`, which creates a core world, sets its focus, ticks it, and reads back its hash, each army's faction and starting strength, its bodies' positions, agent counts and strengths, each army's skills, and each structure's cell, footprint, kind and stage. GDScript cannot construct it; `WorldAdapter.create`, `WorldAdapter.create_m1` and `WorldAdapter.create_region` are the only ways to get one.
- `create_m1` builds the core's M1 benchmark scenario (`core/m1_scenario.cpp`), the same one the tick benchmark runs, and places the focus where the scenario does.
- `create_region` builds the region the core generates from a seed, with one agent born into its first village as the last army, and places the focus on that agent. It keeps what the view draws of the region: each cell's terrain kind and owning faction, the kinds' and factions' names, and the villages, read back by `terrain`, `terrain_names`, `territory`, `faction_names` and `villages`, each village with the name of its template. Its structures carry their kinds, and so do the M1 scenario's, which are barrels. A world from `create` or `create_m1` has no region, and these return empty.
- `order` passes an order to the core: an army, an objective cell as a `Vector2i`, and skill indices as a `PackedInt64Array`. An order the core refuses makes it report the refusal with `push_error` and return false. `map_rows` and `skill_count` read back what a driver needs to aim one.
- Positions cross in sub-cell units as `Vector2`s, exact while a coordinate stays within 2^24. The tick rate, the cell size and the structure stages cross as the class's integer constants, so GDScript repeats none of them.
- `create` takes what the core's constructor takes, as Godot types: a seed, map rows as a `PackedStringArray`, and the armies (with their skills), structures and rules as `Dictionary`s keyed by the core's field names, with cells as `Vector2i`. Converting them to the core's types, and the hash back to a signed int, is the adapter's work.
- Input the core refuses, or that is missing, of the wrong type or outside the core's integer types, makes `create` report it with `push_error` and return null. No exception crosses into Godot.
- It holds no game rule: every call is passed to the core, which decides what is valid.
- `world_art/world_art.tscn` draws a world in the approved art, and both scenes below use it. It holds no game rule.
  - Terrain is laid as `docs/convention/stack-godot.md` §Terrain layers sets out. A world without a region draws its passable cells as plain and its blocked cells as mountain.
  - Territory borders are a dotted line in each owner's accent. Landforms are `Sprite2D`s.
  - Each structure is a `Sprite2D` over its footprint, showing its stage's frame of its kind's art: house, hall, well, barrels or fence. A structure from `create` has no kind and is not drawn.
  - Each village's decorations come from `world_art/villages.json` by its template's name: paving, road and fields as ground tiles on a layer above the terrain, and a banner in its owner's colours standing with everything else. They never block or break.
  - Bodies are drawn only on screen, through one `MultiMesh` per row of cells, from one texture holding every faction's sprites. A body of one agent is its faction's sprite, walking and facing the way it moved since the last tick; a body of several is its faction's aggregate-army marker.
  - The rows, the structures and the landforms are sorted together by the bottom edge of what they stand on. The steered agent carries the player's ring and chevron.
- `world_view/world_view.tscn` shows the M1 scenario through `world_art.tscn`, sets the camera's centre as the focus before each tick, and ticks at the core's tick rate whatever the frame rate. `make bench` renders it.
- The view renders at 480 x 270 and scales to the window by whole multiples, drawing textures without filtering.
- The world is presented in a three-quarter top-down view, one core cell to one 16-pixel tile, with combat in real time on the core's tick. Ground and roofs are seen from above; facades and standing bodies are drawn upright and sorted by the bottom edge of their footprint. A structure is drawn over the footprint of width x height cells the core gives it, which `structure_footprint` reads back; `create` takes no footprint, so every structure it makes is 1 x 1. Art is pixel art on that grid, from the Aseprite sources under `art/`, made to `docs/art-direction.md`. UI text is Korean first, with English supported.
- `play/play.tscn`, the project's main scene, steers the agent `create_region` bears. A left click orders it toward the cell clicked, keys 1 to 9 order it to strike with that skill, and 0 with all its skills; an order keeps whatever part the input does not change. Before each tick it sets the focus on the agent's lead body, and its camera follows. The focus is set this way so that a recorded order log, replayed under the same rule, gives the same hash. It draws the world through `world_art.tscn` and the HUD through `hud/hud.tscn`.
- `hud/hud.tscn` shows the player's portrait and faction; health as current over starting strength and inner energy as a dash, each over its bar; a map of the region around the player; a rumour log; and the technique bar, one slot per skill with its number key and an icon of its shape, `burst` when its radius reaches past its target and `strike` otherwise. It draws in Galmuri9. Its words come from `hud/hud.csv` through Godot's translations: Korean at start, and F2 switches between Korean and English.
- A headless Godot test shows that a world of two hostile armies with skills, and structures, driven through the adapter, gives the core's hash, body positions and counts for the same scenario, focus and tick count, against `world_hash`, a program that runs the core with no Godot; and that invalid scenarios return null while a valid one does not. It also shows that an agent ordered through the adapter leaves home and strikes a hostile camp down, while one left without an order does neither. A second test shows `play.tscn` turning a click and keys into orders: a click on a passable cell moves the agent toward it, and a click on a blocked cell and a key for a skill the agent lacks are refused. A third shows `world_art.tscn` laying the plain on every cell, and each dual-grid tile with the corner mask of its four cells. It also checks that every body on screen and none off it is drawn, that each structure shows its stage's frame of its kind's art, and that each village's ground decorations cover the cells its template's data names. A fourth shows the HUD's words in Korean, then in English after a switch, its health and inner-energy values, and an icon for each skill.

## Rationale

- Godot is the presentation adapter and the core reaches it through GDExtension: ADR 0002 (`adr/0002-build-on-godot-with-a-cpp-simulation-core.md`).
- The binding release, its pin, the build path, the scenario format, how refusals reach GDScript, the test runner and how CI installs Godot: ADR 0004 (`adr/0004-bind-the-core-to-godot-with-godot-cpp-through-cmake.md`).
- Steering through the orders every army follows, and the focus set on the steered body before each tick: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).
- The three-quarter top-down view, structure footprints, real-time combat, pixel art on a 16-pixel tile grid and Korean-first UI text, and the alternatives rejected: ADR 0012 (`adr/0012-present-the-world-in-a-three-quarter-top-down-view.md`), which supersedes ADR 0008 (`adr/0008-present-the-world-top-down-in-real-time-as-pixel-art.md`).

## Open questions

- **Inner energy and the calendar.** The core has neither. The HUD's inner-energy bar stands full with a dash for its value, and its season and year are a fixed placeholder, until the core holds them.
- **Rumours.** The HUD's rumour log shows a placeholder line until the core spreads rumours (`docs/concept.md` §Reputation and rumors).

- **Which platforms the library is built for.** It is built for the host only, macOS arm64 and Linux x86_64 so far; Android and iOS exports are not built.
