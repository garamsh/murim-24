# World generation

How the core generates a region from a seed: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- `core/region.cpp` generates a region from a seed and three data tables, terrain kinds, factions and village templates, and carries the rules of life to the world. `region_data()` returns the game's tables, each defined there once; the generator reads only what it is passed, so a table changes without the generator changing.
- A region is the inputs a `World` is created from (seed, map rows, armies, structures, rules, leadership, life) and what the world does not hold: the terrain kind and owning faction of each cell, and the villages.
- A terrain kind is a name, whether bodies may stand on it, and a weight. The map rows mark the cells of an impassable kind as blocked.
- A faction is a name, the factions it attacks, and the skills its armies use; the rules' hostility matrix is built from those lists. It may carry a succession rule: the Demonic Cult changes leader by duel to the death, and the old leader's allies turn on the winner; the Orthodox and Unorthodox carry none yet. Each faction is led by the first resident of its first village, which the region gives as each faction's leadership. Its skills decide who rebuilds: the Orthodox and Unorthodox guard and mend, and the Demonic Cult guards and torches, rebuilding nothing. A village's residents take their faction's skills.
- The rules of life are five disciplines, sword, saber, fist, hidden weapons and medicine, each drawn alike: a growth of 5 to 15 and a limit of 300 to 700. People live 45 to 75 years, a day longer for each point of attainment, and are born again with the residents' health. A past life widens a practised discipline's limit by 200 each way, and every limit's top by 100, each over the lives lived.
- Kinship and names are part of the rules of life. People the region starts with are 16 to 40 years old. A person of 16 to 40 marries, at even odds a year, someone of another family from a village whose centre lies within 48 cells; a wife of 18 to 40 bears a child at 2 in 5 a year, two years apart at least, into her husband's family. Families draw among 24 surnames, common and of the old houses, and people among 12 given names for each sex, in Korean with romanised forms. A day is 1,800 ticks.
- Tests live the region through 30 years with days of one tick: families reach a third generation, every child is born to a married man and woman in the father's family, and the living stay within 500; without kinship no one marries. With every wife bearing each day she may, the living meet 500 and hold there. Every person is named, a child bears its father's surname, and the same seed gives the same names.
- A table of structure kinds gives each kind what it holds and whether it blocks: a house shelters 8 and a hall 12, a granary stores 2,400 measures of food and a storehouse 3,600, and each cell of a field yields 600 a harvest and blocks nothing. The region keeps what each structure holds beside it, and `village_specs` passes it on. Each person eats a measure a day; unfed for ten days, a person moves to a village within 64 cells with room and food, or dies. A village of 26 founders is capped at 50, a hamlet of 12 at 20.
- Tests show a village's cap as the least of housing, food and storage, a burnt field feeding no one, a village without a harvest emptying into a neighbour with room or dying without one, and, with every wife bearing as she may, villages filling to their caps and never past them; without husbandry they pass them.
- Combat is paced by the tables' data, at 30 ticks a second. Every person has 120 health. The guard strike does 10 damage within 3 cells every 15 ticks, the torch 10 to structures and the mend 5 back every 30 ticks, and a structure has 10,000 durability, with stage floors of 10,000, 5,000 and 1. The targets:
  - a duel to the death, from the challenge, in 10 to 30 seconds
  - twenty a side, from two cells apart on an open field, until one side falls, in 10 seconds to 2 minutes
  - a band of twenty raiders leaving a hamlet in ruins in 1 to 5 minutes
  - a band of 44, the hamlet's scaled to the village's 26 residents, leaving the walled village in ruins in 1 to 10 minutes
- Tests measure each target on the generated region over three seeds; the old placeholder strike of 1, and a torch of a tenth the damage, overrun them. A band of twenty, which the walled village outnumbers, still strikes its residents down; kept where it stands, it strikes no one.
- `village_specs` gives the villages as a World is created with them: each village's structures and resident army.
- A village template is a name, the area it occupies, width x height cells, and its buildings. Each building is a structure with a footprint and its top-left cell as an offset within that area, the kind of building it is (`house`, `hall`, `well`, `barrels`, `fence`), and the residents the village starts with in it: 4 for a house, 6 for a hall, none for the rest. A template's buildings lie inside its area, do not overlap, leave its centre free and house at least one resident. A village has a name, the name of its template, an owner, its area, its structures and a resident army of as many people as its buildings start with, standing on the open cells nearest its centre, bound for the centre; their home is the village. The region keeps each structure's kind beside it.
- The templates lay out the approved mockup's village. Wells, barrels and the fence are structures, so they block movement and break like the houses. The mockup's fields of millet and rice are structures of the `field` kind, which block no movement and burn under a torch; the village adds a granary east of its square and a storehouse in its south-east corner, and the hamlet keeps its millet and adds a granary in its south-west corner. The paving, road and banner never block or break; they are presentation data in `world_art/villages.json`, keyed by template name.
- The game's two templates follow the approved target mockup (`art/mockup/target_screen.lua`): the village is its 17 x 9 cells, houses of 3 x 3 and a hall of 5 x 3 north of the road through its centre and two houses south of it; the hamlet is its west end, 7 x 9 cells with three houses.
- Terrain and territory each partition the grid by nearest site: terrain around sites at random cells, each of a kind drawn by weight; territory around one capital per faction, each the farthest from those already placed among a fixed number of random cells.
- `born_in` makes an army of one person born into a village: of the village's faction, on the first open cell of its area, row by row, that no resident stands on, with the residents' skills and health. The player's body is born this way.
- Six to eight villages are owned in turn by each faction. Each takes a template drawn by the seed among those with room, and a corner drawn among every corner where that template's area lies on free passable cells of its owner's territory; a summed-area table of those cells tests each corner. No template with room is a failure the generator reports.
- Generation uses integers only, and the seed is its only source of randomness, so the same seed and tables give the same region. The draws come from the same generator as the M1 scenario's (`core/random.cpp`).
- Tests over 200 seeds show every village on passable cells in its owner's territory, its structures inside it and off its residents' cells, no two villages overlapping, every region a valid world, and a fourth faction added to the table alone generated with territory and villages. A test shows each structure carrying its building's kind and each resident army as many people as its village's buildings start with.

## Rationale

- Templates of footprints from the approved mockup, a template drawn among those with room, and the region at 128 cells: ADR 0013 (`adr/0013-give-structures-footprints-that-block-movement-in-every-stage.md`).
- Content as data tables, nearest-site partitions, capitals spread by best candidate, and villages placed among every corner that fits: ADR 0009 (`adr/0009-generate-a-region-from-data-tables-by-nearest-site-partitions.md`).
- The rules of life as one table the world is given: ADR 0019 (`adr/0019-draw-talent-and-lifespan-at-birth-and-bear-a-soul-again.md`).
- Residents as people with the village as their home, a cell apart: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`).
- A person born into a village as an army of one, the shape the player's body takes: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).
- Integers only, with the seed as the only randomness, for determinism across builds: ADR 0005 (`adr/0005-simulate-armies-at-three-levels-of-detail-in-fixed-point.md`).

## Open questions

- **Whether territory should follow terrain.** Territory ignores terrain, so a border can cross a mountain range, and a faction's territory can be mostly impassable. No seed tested lacks room for a village, but nothing guarantees it.
- **How large a region the game needs.** The region is 128 cells square, the smallest size tested at which every one of 200 seeds held room for its villages with four factions; it is not measured against play.
