# World generation

How the core generates a region from a seed: what exists of it now, and the decisions it rests on.

## Current decisions

- `core/region.cpp` generates a region from a seed and three data tables, terrain kinds, factions and village templates, and carries the rules of life to the world. `region_data()` returns the game's tables, each defined there once; the generator reads only what it is passed, so a table changes without the generator changing.
- A region is the inputs a `World` is created from (seed, map rows, armies, structures, rules, leadership, life) and what the world does not hold: the terrain kind and owning faction of each cell, and the villages.
- A terrain kind is a name, its footing and a weight. Its footing is buildable, rough, which bodies cross and nothing is built on, or blocked, and the map rows mark each cell with its kind's, `.`, `^` or `#`. Plain, of weight 10, and forest, of 3, are buildable; hills, of 2, are rough; water and mountains, of 1 each, are blocked. Landforms stand only on hills and mountains, so none stands where anything is built.
- A faction is a name, the factions it attacks, and the skills its armies use; the rules' hostility matrix is built from those lists. It may carry a succession rule: the Demonic Cult changes leader by duel to the death, and the old leader's allies turn on the winner; the Orthodox and Unorthodox carry none yet. Each faction is led by the first resident of its first village, which the region gives as each faction's leadership. Its skills decide who rebuilds: the Orthodox and Unorthodox guard and mend, and the Demonic Cult guards and torches, rebuilding nothing. A village's residents take their faction's skills.
- Each faction carries who its sects admit and whether it elects its leader. Sects admit their faction's people of 12 years or more whose home lies within 48 cells of the seat, at 3 in 10 a year: the Orthodox and the Demonic Cult a talent limit of 550 or more in a discipline, the Unorthodox, proof of usefulness, an attainment of 20. The third of a sect's members of the highest attainment are its elders, at least one. The Orthodox elect their leader, with no term; the others do not.
- Each faction has one sect, Hwasan for the Orthodox, the Heavenly Demon for the Demonic Cult and the Black Fang for the Unorthodox, each holding the manual of its art: the sword, the saber and hidden weapons. A fallen sect's manuals scatter at even odds within 16 cells of its seat, and are otherwise lost. A sect's seat is a village laid out from the seat template, 12 x 9 cells, in its faction's territory, after the other villages: a house for its founding household, a manual hall and a hall along its north side, a training ground of 6 x 4 cells in its south-west corner, a granary and a field of rice beside it with a cell between them, and the seat gate on its south edge below that cell. Growth keeps clear its court, the two rows before its halls, the path from the court between the granary and the field to the gate, and the two rows before the gate outside the seat. Its founders are the seat's residents, the first of them at an attainment of 120, so it is its sect's master.

- The rules of life are five disciplines, sword, saber, fist, hidden weapons and medicine, each drawn alike: a growth of 5 to 15 and a limit of 300 to 700. People live 45 to 75 years, a day longer for each point of attainment, and are born again with the residents' health. A past life widens a practised discipline's limit by 200 each way, and every limit's top by 100, each over the lives lived.
- Kinship and names are part of the rules of life. People the region starts with are 16 to 28 years old. A person of 16 to 40 marries, at even odds a year, someone of another family from a village whose centre lies within 80 cells; a wife of 18 to 40 bears a child at 2 in 5 a year, two years apart at least, into her husband's family, of the sex fewer among her village's unmarried who are not past marrying age. Families draw among 24 surnames, common and of the old houses, and people among 12 given names for each sex, in Korean with romanised forms. A day is 1,800 ticks.
- An event reaches everyone within 32 cells of it as a rumour, a quarter of the region's side, the rules' `rumour_reach`.
- Tests live the region through 30 years with days of one tick: families reach a third generation, every child is born to a married man and woman in the father's family or, once it founds a village, a branch bearing its surname, and the living stay within 500; without kinship no one marries but the households the region starts with. With every wife bearing each day she may, from villages as full as grown ones, the living meet 500 and hold there. Every person is named, a child bears its father's surname, and the same seed gives the same names.
- A table of structure kinds gives each kind what it holds and whether it blocks: a house shelters 8 and a hall 12, a granary stores 2,400 measures of food and a storehouse 3,600, and each cell of a field yields 600 a harvest and blocks nothing. A hall and a manual hall each teach 4 pupils and a training ground 12, on which people stand, so it blocks nothing. A smithy of 3 x 2 cells arms 40 people, who strike a quarter harder, and an apothecary of 4 x 2 cells tends 40, who recover 12 health a day. The region keeps what each structure holds beside it, and `village_specs` passes it on. Each person eats a measure a day; unfed for ten days, a person moves to a village within 48 cells with room and food, or dies. A village is capped at 50, a hamlet at 20.
- Tests show a village's cap as the least of housing, food and storage, a burnt field feeding no one, a village without a harvest emptying into a neighbour with room or dying without one, and, with every wife bearing as she may, no growth, and villages as full as grown ones, villages filling to their caps and never past them; without husbandry they pass them.
- Growth is part of the rules of life, and each faction carries its stance. The Orthodox build first, then found, then raid; the Unorthodox found first, then raid, then build; the Demonic Cult only raids, since its torch raises nothing. Every 30 days a village at a fifth of its cap grows:
  - it builds a house, a field of rice, a granary, a smithy or an apothecary on buildable cells within 8 cells of its centre; it arms and tends 20 people with no workshop, so a hamlet, capped at 20, builds neither workshop, and a village, at 50, builds both
  - or it sends 6 settlers to lay out a hamlet, the template's buildings as the layout, on buildable land within 40 cells and 3 cells clear of any structure
  - or it sends 10 raiders against a hostile village within 40 cells

  A village holds the land within 16 cells as territory. `region_data` builds the growth's plans from the structure kinds and the hamlet template, and names the hamlet as the kind of village the settlement makes; `generate_region` gives each faction's stance to it.
- A test lives the region through 50 years with days of five ticks: villages are founded and one comes to house its settlers, a building is raised, a raiding party is sent, the factions' territories change, the living stay within 500, and the same seed grows the same way. With no faction taking a stance, no village is founded and no party sent. Another lives seed 2026 through 100 years: villages raise a smithy and an apothecary; with self-reliance of 500 they raise other buildings and no workshop.
- Each faction starts with three or four households, its seat's included, at most four, over 200 seeds; villages as full as grown ones start with more. A test lives seed 2026 through 100 years with days of five ticks: the living rise from 20 to at least three times as many, the villages, founded ones included, at least double, every faction keeps a peopled village at the end of every year, and the living stay within 500. Its sects admit members beyond their founders, a master changes, the Orthodox are led by another after their leader dies, and each standing sect ends with at least one elder and at most a third of its members. Without admission no sect admits anyone and no master changes or is elected; without growth no village is founded, though births still fill the villages there are; without kinship the living do not rise. A test lives seed 9, without sects, through 100 years the same way: with balanced sexes, every faction keeps a peopled village and the living at least double; with sexes drawn at even odds, its first children are nearly all daughters, a faction dies out and the living do not double.
- Combat is paced by the tables' data, at 30 ticks a second. Every person has 120 health. The guard strike does 10 damage within 3 cells every 15 ticks, the torch 10 to structures and the mend 5 back every 30 ticks, and a structure has 10,000 durability, with stage floors of 10,000, 5,000 and 1. The targets:
  - a duel to the death, from the challenge, in 10 to 30 seconds
  - twenty a side, from two cells apart on an open field, until one side falls, in 10 seconds to 2 minutes
  - a band of twenty raiders leaving a hamlet in ruins in 1 to 5 minutes
  - a band of 44, the hamlet's scaled to the village's 26 residents, leaving the walled village in ruins in 1 to 10 minutes

  Each target is measured on villages as full as grown ones: the hamlet with 6 households, 12 people, and the walled village with 13, 26 people.
- Tests measure each target on the generated region over three seeds; the old placeholder strike of 1, and a torch of a tenth the damage, overrun them. A band of twenty, which the walled village outnumbers, still strikes its residents down; kept where it stands, it strikes no one.
- `village_specs` gives the villages as a World is created with them: each village's structures, resident army and the households its residents start as.
- A village template is a name, the area it occupies, width x height cells, and its buildings. Each building is a structure with a footprint and its top-left cell as an offset within that area, the kind of building it is (`house`, `hall`, `well`, `barrels`, `fence`), and the households the village starts with in it, each a married man and woman: one in the hamlet's first house and one in the village's hall, none in the rest. A template's buildings lie inside its area, do not overlap, leave its centre free and house at least one household. A village has a name, the name of its template, an owner, its area, its structures, its households and a resident army of two people for each household, standing on the open cells nearest its centre, bound for the centre; their home is the village. The region keeps each structure's kind beside it.
- The templates lay out the approved mockup's village. Wells, barrels and the fence are structures, so they block movement and break like the houses. The mockup's fields of millet and rice are structures of the `field` kind, which block no movement and burn under a torch; the village adds a granary east of its square and a storehouse in its south-east corner, and the hamlet keeps its millet and adds a granary in its south-west corner. The paving, road and banner never block or break; they are presentation data in `world_art/villages.json`, keyed by template name.
- The game's two templates follow the approved target mockup (`art/mockup/target_screen.lua`): the village is its 17 x 9 cells, houses of 3 x 3 and a hall of 5 x 3 north of the road through its centre and two houses south of it; the hamlet is its west end, 7 x 9 cells with three houses.
- Terrain and territory each partition the grid by nearest site: terrain around sites at random cells, each of a kind drawn by weight; territory around one capital per faction, each the farthest from those already placed among a fixed number of random cells.
- `born_in` makes an army of one person born into a village: of the village's faction, on the first open cell of its area, row by row, that no resident stands on, with the residents' skills and health. The player's body is born this way.
- Six to eight villages are owned in turn by each faction, and each sect's seat is laid out after them at a corner drawn among every corner where the seat template lies on free buildable cells of its faction's territory. Each takes a template drawn by the seed among those with room, and a corner drawn among every corner where that template's area lies on free buildable cells of its owner's territory, as `Map::buildable` reads the map rows; a summed-area table of those cells tests each corner. No template with room is a failure the generator reports.
- Generation uses integers only, and the seed is its only source of randomness, so the same seed and tables give the same region. The draws come from the same generator as the M1 scenario's (`core/random.cpp`).
- Tests over 200 seeds show every village on buildable cells in its owner's territory, a rough cell under one failing the check, its structures inside it and off its residents' cells, no two villages overlapping, every region a valid world, and a fourth faction added to the table alone generated with territory and villages. A test shows each structure carrying its building's kind and each resident army as many people as its village's buildings start with. Another shows each seat handing the world its template's clearings, every cell of them inside the seat open, while a village keeps none.

## Rationale

- Templates of footprints from the approved mockup, a template drawn among those with room, and the region at 128 cells: ADR 0013 (`adr/0013-give-structures-footprints-that-block-movement-in-every-stage.md`).
- Each terrain kind's footing as data, read by one test for villages and growth alike, and the plain's weight of 10: ADR 0024 (`adr/0024-build-only-on-terrain-whose-kind-bears-buildings.md`). Over seeds 0 to 199, plain and forest buildable at that weight leave every seed room for its villages with three factions and with four; the plain alone at its old weight of 4 left 26 and 44 seeds without, and plain and forest at 4 left 2 and 4.
- Content as data tables, nearest-site partitions, capitals spread by best candidate, and villages placed among every corner that fits: ADR 0009 (`adr/0009-generate-a-region-from-data-tables-by-nearest-site-partitions.md`).
- The rules of life as one table the world is given: ADR 0019 (`adr/0019-draw-talent-and-lifespan-at-birth-and-bear-a-soul-again.md`).
- The rumour reach, and the ring of events rumours are read from: ADR 0032 (`adr/0032-keep-a-bounded-ring-of-world-events-and-read-rumours-from-it.md`).
- Sects, their seats as villages, admission, the election and manuals: ADR 0026 (`adr/0026-keep-sect-membership-on-the-person-and-seat-each-sect-in-a-village.md`). Elders as a third of each sect's members: ADR 0028 (`adr/0028-make-a-sects-elders-the-share-of-its-strongest-members.md`).
- Children of the sex their village's unmarried lack: ADR 0027 (`adr/0027-bear-each-child-of-the-sex-its-village-lacks.md`). Over 100 years with days of five ticks, every faction keeps a peopled village on all of seeds 0 to 49 (42 with sexes at even odds) and on 48 of seeds 50 to 99 (44), and the living at least double on every one.
- Villages starting from households of married couples, each heading a family of its own, and the growth data tuned to start small: ADR 0025 (`adr/0025-start-each-village-from-households-of-married-couples.md`). Over 100 years with days of five ticks, seeds 24, 2026 and 7 go from 12, 14 and 14 living to 97, 123 and 125, and from 6, 7 and 7 peopled villages to 17, 18 and 19; with households but the old growth data, no village is founded.
- Residents as people with the village as their home, a cell apart: ADR 0017 (`adr/0017-make-every-npc-an-individual-and-every-army-a-roster.md`).
- A person born into a village as an army of one, the shape the player's body takes: ADR 0011 (`adr/0011-steer-a-body-by-the-orders-every-army-follows.md`).
- Integers only, with the seed as the only randomness, for determinism across builds: ADR 0005 (`adr/0005-simulate-armies-at-three-levels-of-detail-in-fixed-point.md`).

## Open questions

- **Room for seats with more factions.** Every one of 200 seeds has room for the three seats with three factions. A fourth faction added as data, with the sects unchanged, leaves a seat no room on 5 of 200 seeds, so the test that adds one does so without sects.
- **Whether territory should follow terrain.** Territory ignores terrain, so a border can cross a mountain range, and a faction's territory can be mostly impassable. No seed tested lacks room for a village, but nothing guarantees it.
- **How large a region the game needs.** The region is 128 cells square, the smallest size tested at which every one of 200 seeds held room for its villages with four factions; it is not measured against play.
